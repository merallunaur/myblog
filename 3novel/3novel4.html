Aiú, Arthur. Aiú，亞瑟。
If this body of water were as tame as he was, submitting to its riverbed, he would turn and leave the rainforest, walking into another hard, grey wilderness. But before his eyes, countless ripples tangled, separated, and tangled again under the sun. He was still unskilled at rowing alone; he raised the paddle high and smashed the water surface like a hammer. Sweat fell with his raised arms into the black tributary of the Rio Negro.① Stirring the riverbed, the faint acidity like aged black tea reminded him of his homeland beyond the Atlantic. The creamy peach sweetness of the violet cattleya rested on the tip of his nose; when he turned his neck to search for it, there was nothing to find. The dogbane vine brushed his hat. The Victoria amazonica leaves were too wide; he had to push them aside with the paddle. The thorns of the shallow-water arrowhead snagged threads from his cuff. Sometimes he stopped paddling and used his fountain pen to record names like spells and magical fragrances. Tabernaemontana②—the word he remembered most deeply. A language that drifted in from across the Atlantic and was born alongside the steps of samba. He raised his hand, picked a flower, and tucked it into his notebook. The approaching humid heat still filled him with fear; his skin was burned by the mildly toxic sap, bringing a scorching heat. The resin flowing from pruned plants, the nectar, the green bitterness of broken leaves, mixed with the dry paper smell of the specimen press—the perfume exclusive to botanists. Aiú，亞瑟。
His camp was deeper in the rainforest. Along the way he saw many trees marked with signs. The sound of saws seemed close at hand. Illegal logging, ubiquitous as ants, forced him to explore farther. At dawn he set up his boat amid birdsong and rowed into the river; the mental haze brought by the high fever should have kept him in his tent. 如果這片水域如他一般溫馴、臣服於河床，他就會轉身離開雨林，走入另一片堅硬灰暗的莽原中去。而他眼前無數片水波在太陽底下交纏、分離、再交纏。他對獨自划船還不熟練，高舉著槳葉，像一把錘子似的砸開水面。汗液隨著他抬起的手臂落入內格羅河①黑色的支流。攪動河底，陳年紅茶似的微酸會使他想起大西洋外的故土。堇花卡特蘭散發的奶油蜜桃甜花香停在他的鼻尖上，當他轉動脖子找尋時，卻無物可求。狗牙花藤蔓撫過他的帽子。王蓮的葉子太寬，他不得不用槳撥向一邊去。淺水澤瀉的刺把他的袖口勾出絲來。有時他停下槳，用鋼筆記錄下各種宛如咒語的名字與魔法般的香氣。Tabernaemontana②，他記憶最深的一個詞。從大西洋上飄來，又在桑巴腳步中伴生的語言。他抬起手，摘下一朵夾入筆記本。迫近的潮熱仍使他心生恐懼，皮膚被含有輕微毒素的汁液灼傷，帶來一陣燙熱。修剪植物流出的樹脂、花蜜、破損葉片的青澀氣，混合紙質標本夾的乾燥紙味，是植物學家專屬的香水。
Every midnight in the rainforest he had to light a lamp to drive away snakes. Last midnight he suddenly woke; the lamp had gone out at some point, and a boa constrictor had poked its head out from under the tent. Arthur saw it and met its gaze. It showed no intention of attacking. It swayed its head, turned, and swam back into the dense forest. Arthur swallowed. The rainforest was more absurd than he had imagined; death here was as easy as breaking glass. The rainforest was also gentler than he had imagined—there were too many living things, and you would always feel someone watching your back. After being interrupted, he could hardly sleep again. From the symphony composed by tens of thousands of insects and animals, his ears caught a familiar melody. Like a man calling, or a woman, sometimes a child, a dog. Occasionally he heard his own name, but took it for the friction between tree crowns. Closing his eyes, he dozed fitfully for another two or three hours. The sound did not disappear. So he chose to pick up the paddle and slip deeper into the rainforest. Following the direction of that sound. In fact, the sound had no direction; it seemed to come from all sides. Arthur acted on his own instinct. This was hallucination; he did not want to call it fate. 他的營地在雨林更深處。沿路他看見不少畫著記號的樹。鋸聲彷彿近在咫尺。如螞蟻一般無處不在的盜伐活動，逼著他向遠探索。清早他在鳥鳴聲中支起船划入河流，高熱帶來的精神恍惚本該使他待在帳篷裡。
Aiú, Arthur. Aiú，亞瑟。
At this moment, his palm left a wet mark on the paper; ink as deep as river water dripped into small black fish, swimming across the page. He was sunk in his own thoughts when a woodcreeper flew onto his head and urgently tapped his hat brim with its beak. Hey, hey, I'm not a tree. Arthur reached out; the bird hopped onto his hand, tilted its head to stare at him, and let out a wet, muffled, melancholy whistle. The tail note was like a firework falling into water, cut short in a long, piercing cry. It seemed to want to tell him something, but he was used to this. Some birds foolishly approach humans and ultimately meet their own destruction. Arthur raised his hand, lifting the bird into the gap between the tree crowns. It circled above his head for a while, then finally gave up and flew into the distance. A smile broke across Arthur's perpetually tense face. He did not notice that the riverbed led straight to a cliff. By the time he came to his senses, it was too late. He rowed desperately backward, but the current only pushed him forward faster. In his last vision remained white water splashing like a bird. He bent down in the rapids, curling his body to protect his head and neck. The boat leaped out of the water and spun down like a swan shot by a bullet. His temple struck the bank, and everything went black. 此刻，他的掌心在紙上留下濕痕，顏色如河水般深的墨水滴落成黑色的小魚，從紙上遊過。他正沉在自己的思緒裡，一隻砍林鳥飛上他的頭頂，用鳥喙急匆匆地敲著他的帽簷。嘿，嘿我不是一棵樹。亞瑟伸出手，鳥兒跳上他的手，歪著頭盯著他，發出濕悶的憂鬱長哨。尾音如掉入水中的煙花，在久久的尖嘯中戛然而止。它像是要告訴他什麼事，而他卻對此習以為常。有些鳥兒會傻傻地親近人類，最後迎來自身的毀滅。亞瑟將手向上抬，將鳥托入樹冠間的縫隙中。它在他頭頂又盤旋了一會兒，最後放棄似的飛向遠處。亞瑟一直緊繃著的臉上綻開笑容，他沒有注意到河床正直直地通向斷崖。等他回過神為時已晚，拼命向後划，水流也只將他更快地推向前方。他最後的視覺裡殘存著飛濺如鳥的白色水花，在急流中彎下腰，捲起身護住頭頸。船跳出水面，又像被子彈射中的天鵝般旋轉著落下。他的額角磕在岸邊，眼前一片漆黑。
"I am not a tree." 「我不是一棵樹。」
Something tapped his forehead, once, twice. Had the woodcreeper come back? The moment he opened his eyes, a kaleidoscopic halo flashed through Arthur's too-clear eyes, and unprepared, he met another pair of eyes. Those eyes looked at him deeply, then curved slightly, like climbing to the high canopy and seeing the moon of the southern hemisphere. Arthur bounced up in fright, but collided with the other's forehead. 有東西一下一下敲著他的額頭。砍林鳥飛回來了嗎？睜眼的瞬間，亞瑟那雙太過清澈的眼中閃過一陣萬花筒似的光暈，毫無準備地對上另一雙眼睛。那雙眼睛深深地看著他，接著微微彎起來，就像爬上高樹層，看見南半球的月亮那般。亞瑟嚇得彈起來，卻撞到了那人的額頭。
"Who are you?" 「你是誰？」
Rubbing his head, which had received a double pain, he put distance between himself and the other. The stranger's eyes bloomed like lotuses when they gazed at you, then closed again. His limbs were as long as rivers, his skin shining with the hard lustre of minerals. His long hair was tied behind his head. The sun was falling from overhead, wrapping him in radiance. He wore work clothes similar to Arthur's and tilted his head, looking at him in confusion. Arthur asked many questions in succession and received no response. The stranger helped him up, spread his hands facing the lake surface. He opened his mouth and uttered his first syllable. 他一边揉着自己迎来双重疼痛的脑袋，一边与对方拉开距离。陌生人的眼睛会在凝视你时如莲花一般绽放，又合拢。四肢如河流般修长，皮肤闪着矿物质的坚硬光泽。他的长发束在脑后，太阳正从头顶落下，将他笼罩在光辉中。他穿着与亚瑟差不多的工装，困惑地歪头看着他。亚瑟接连询问很多句，没有得到任何回应。陌生人将他扶起来，摊开手面对着湖面。他张开嘴，发出第一个音节。
"Aiú." 「Aiú.」

"Where is your camp?" 「你的營地在哪邊？」
Francis raised his hand and pointed deep into the jungle. Arthur looked in vain, his gaze struggling within the green egg. 弗朗西斯抬起手，指向叢林深處。亞瑟徒勞張望，目光在綠色的卵中掙扎。
"Then why did you come?" 「那你是為什麼來？」
Francis squinted and smiled at him. His gaze lingered on Arthur's face for a moment, then cast into the jungle behind him. In his eyes remained a long, silent peace, yet intertwined with a curious light. The day after bandaging Arthur's wound, Francis appeared before him carrying a huge backpack and pitched his camp beside his. He had a notebook similar to Arthur's; opening it released a special fragrance. Yet he had an even more sensitive perception than Arthur. He could close his eyes and immediately find the tree he wanted. The way he approached a tree was always wonderful. His steps would first slow, and when he touched the nearest buttress root, he pressed his palm against it. That hand seemed to be caressing a child, sliding from the roots all the way to the trunk. Then he parted the epiphytic vines on the trunk and pressed his ear against it with closed eyes. His breathing would gradually become soft, then inaudible, like the last breath exhaled from the mouth of a dead man. His breath merged into this tree. 弗朗西斯對著他瞇起眼睛笑。目光在亞瑟臉上流連一瞬，又投入他身後的叢林。他的眼神中長久地留存著一種寂靜的平和，卻又與好奇的光芒交相輝映。為亞瑟包紮傷口後的第二天，弗朗西斯背著巨大的行李背包出現在他面前，將營地扎在他旁邊。他有一個跟他差不多的筆記本，一打開就發出一陣特殊香氣。他卻又有比他更加敏感的知覺，他能閉著眼睛，馬上找到想要的樹木。他接近一棵樹的方式總是很奇妙。腳步會先變得緩慢，當接觸到離他最近的一塊板狀根時，便把掌心貼上去。那隻手像是在撫摸孩子那般，一路從根系滑到樹幹。旋即撥開樹幹上的附生藤，閉著眼睛把耳朵貼上去。他的呼吸會漸漸變得輕柔，接著再也聽不見，就像死人口中吐出的最後一口氣。他的呼吸融進了這棵樹。
Arthur preferred to approach this place in a rational way. He always used a knife to shave off a piece of bark and stuck it into his notebook with transparent tape. Even though the colour of his eyes was optically rooted in the same source as the colour of the rainforest, his eyes could not pierce into this soil that was, to him, too barren. Rather, he feared this rainforest—he did not fear the deadly poisonous insects and frogs that could take his life, but he feared the rainforest's gaze. When everything was still, he would feel thousands of eyes pressed against his back, only the moisture leaving a wet kiss on his eyelids. Here, apart from living things, there was nothing. Sometimes, he also avoided Francis's gaze. His gaze, and the rainforest's gaze, looked the same. 亞瑟更願意以一種理性的方式靠近這個地方。他總是用刀削去一道樹皮，用透明膠貼在筆記本中。即便他眼睛的顏色，與雨林的顏色在光學上同根同源，他的眼睛也扎不進這片對他來說太過貧瘠的土壤。不如說他恐懼這片雨林——他並不恐懼這裡致命的毒蟲與蛙奪走他的生命，卻害怕雨林的注視。當一切靜止不動，他會感到幾千雙眼睛貼在他的背上，只有水汽在他眼皮上留下濕潤的吻。這裡除了生物，一無所有。有時，他也一樣會逃避弗朗西斯的注視。他的注視，與雨林的注視，看起來是一樣的。
When vines or roots tangled around his ankles, Francis would bend his elbow and catch his palm stained with blood-like sap. An anchor fell from the clouds of the rainforest. Francis. Arthur did not actually know his name; apart from that syllable from his dream, whose meaning he did not know, he had never spoken another word. He chose to name him religiously—Francis.③ The one who walked alone into the deep mountains to soothe the man-eating wolf, the one who preached to the birds on the mountain. All things loved him; his body had never borne the mark of a bullet of rejection. So complete, carrying a joy unstained by original sin. The joy of creation, pure admiration and the joy of sunlight. Yet his hands were also covered with burns and knife scars. After wandering the rainforest, what chance does a parrot have of finding its way home? What chance does a person have of meeting another person? Regardless of the chance of meeting, humans and living things will ultimately be forced apart for the same reason. But he could not face the gaze alone, so he chose to accompany Francis. That abundant, ethereal, summoning forest had already bitten into his shadow. 當藤蔓或根纏住他的腳踝，弗朗西斯就會屈起手肘，接住他沾著如血般樹汁的手掌，雨林的雲層中從天而降一根錨。弗朗西斯。亞瑟其實並不知道他叫什麼，除了那個來自他夢中的，不知意義的音節，他從未開口說過其他話。他選擇用宗教為他命名，弗朗西斯③。那個獨自走進深山安撫吃人惡狼的人，那個向滿山飛鳥布道的人。萬物都愛他，他的身上從未有過被拒絕的子彈穿透的痕跡。如此完整，帶著未被原罪浸染過的快樂。創世之樂，純粹的讚嘆與陽光下歡愉的樂。而他的雙手，卻也一樣佈滿灼傷和刀痕。在雨林徘徊後，一隻鸚鵡有多大機率找到歸巢的路？一個人有多大機率遇見另一個人？無論相遇時的機緣，人與生物最終都會因為相同的原因被迫分離。但他無法一個人對抗注視，於是選擇與弗朗西斯結伴。那片豐收般的、縹緲的、充滿召喚的森林，已經咬住了他的影子。

On the plane to Rio, Arthur held his bag tightly, seeking a bit of security. Over the past few weeks, he and Francis had collected many plant samples to send back to the research institute in Rio. Through the porthole, the rainforest grew farther and farther behind. Arthur's mood lightened a little. The sun sank into the water; the clouds burned fiery red. Staring at the clouds too long made his eyes sting from the intense colour. Closing his eyes still left blue-violet afterimages. Arthur lowered the window shade and leaned against the window to rest. A hand suddenly grabbed his wrist. 在前往里約的飛機上，亞瑟緊緊抱著自己的包，以此獲取一點安全感。過去幾個星期，他與弗朗西斯一起採集了不少植物樣本，要送回建在里約的研究所。透過舷窗，雨林在身後越來越遠。亞瑟的心情稍微輕鬆了一點。太陽沉入水中，雲層燒得火紅。盯著雲層看太久，眼睛會被這種濃烈的顏色刺痛。閉上眼睛後仍舊會留下藍紫色的殘像。亞瑟放下遮光板，靠著窗閉目養神，一隻手卻突然抓住了他的手腕。

The deep blue canopy closed its palms from above; his face was hidden in the half-cold, half-warm light, like the boundary between river and forest. Arthur closed his eyes again. The air was filled with the scent of spices, and soft, slow Brazilian Portuguese drifted over. He relaxed his furrowed brow and leaned toward the aisle to sleep. On Avenida Vieira Souto,⑧ two police officers chased a child carrying a beer display rack. Ipanema Beach was packed with people; beer was selling hot, almost gone. An old Brazilian national team number 9 jersey⑨ fluttered on the child like a flag. At first, his round face showed panic. He raised his head and looked at Francis. Arthur was about to retort, but the man stood up, turned, and walked deep into the forest. Arthur wanted to follow, but Francis extended a hand and pressed it against his chest. The man walked a dozen steps, then suddenly stopped. His back blurred for a moment among the ferns and vines, then he waved his hand vigorously into the distance. His arm traced a very slow arc in the air, as if waving goodbye to something. But then the arc froze. His body stiffened in place, his arm suspended in midair. 弗朗西斯沒有收回手，指尖仍然貼著男人的額頭。他轉過臉看了亞瑟一眼，又把目光落回男人臉上，嘴角微微動了一下。亞瑟正要反駁，男人卻站起來，轉過身，朝著林子深處走去。亞瑟想要跟上去，但弗朗西斯伸出一隻手，按在他的胸口。男人走了十幾步，忽然停下來。他的背影在蕨草與藤蔓之間模糊了一瞬，又大力朝遠處揮了一下手。他的手臂在空中劃過一道極緩的弧線，像在揮別什麼。但緊接著那弧線就頓住了。他的身體僵在原地，手臂懸在半空。
A tree in the distance was falling. One after another, like dominoes pushed by an invisible finger. The sound of trunks hitting the ground was dull and long. The roar caught up with the line of sight—the bellow of diesel engines, the shriek of saw teeth biting into wood marrow, the low friction of iron cables dragging trunks over the mud. A swarm of metal, tireless insects gnawed at the bones of this forest. The man stood blankly. His arm slowly dropped; as it dropped, it touched the broken end of a sapling cut in two beside him, his fingertip stained with sap. He looked down at his hand and did not wipe away the wet mark. 遠處一棵樹正在倒下。一棵接一棵，像被看不見的手指推倒的骨牌。樹幹砸在地面上的聲響沉悶而綿長。轟鳴聲追上了視線——柴油引擎的怒吼，鋸齒咬進木髓的尖嘯，鐵索拖拽樹幹時刮過泥土的低沉摩擦。一群金屬的、不知疲倦的昆蟲啃食著這片林子的骨骼。男人愣愣地站著，他的手臂慢慢垂下來，垂下時碰到了身側一株被切成兩半的幼樹的斷口，指尖染上樹液。他低頭看著自己的手，沒有擦去這道濕痕。
Arthur ran over, wanting to block them with his own body. He knew it was futile. But a soft call entered his ear. 亞瑟跑過去，想用自己的身體擋住他們。他清楚這只是徒勞。但一聲輕喚傳入他的耳朵。
"Aiú." 「Aiú.」
Arthur turned back. Francis stood there, without anger—his face did not even hold the peace Arthur was familiar with, the peace that had long remained in the rainforest. It was something emptier, more eternal. Arthur gazed into Francis's eyes; something was changing there, not his pupils or irises, but something deeper, more fundamental, like silt on a riverbed slowly stirred by an undercurrent. Arthur rubbed his eyes—Francis's figure seemed to have become taller, or wider, or more… dense. The change was too rapid. He saw Francis's arms growing darker, thicker; on the surface of his skin, vertical, undulating patterns were emerging, like the grain of tree bark. Arthur wanted to shout, but no sound came. His instinct recognised what was happening earlier than his consciousness. Francis was still standing there, but he was becoming a tree. His feet sank into the soil; roots were tentatively extending downward. His shoulder blades pushed up the back of his work clothes; those two protruding arcs were widening, intertwining with the surrounding aerial roots, vines, epiphytic orchid roots, like a spider weaving itself into the centre of its own web. 亞瑟回過頭，弗朗西斯站在那裡，沒有憤怒——他的臉上甚至沒有那種亞瑟熟悉的、在雨林中長久留存著的平和。那是一種更空、更加永恆的東西。亞瑟注視著弗朗西斯的眼睛，那裡面有東西正在變化，不是他的瞳孔或虹膜，而是更深處、更底層的某種質地，像河床上的淤泥被一股暗流緩緩掀動。亞瑟揉了揉眼睛——弗朗西斯的身形似乎變得更高了，或者更寬了，或者更……密了。那種變化太迅速。他看見弗朗西斯的手臂正在變深，變粗，表皮上正浮現出一道一道縱向的、起伏的紋路，如同樹皮的走向。亞瑟想要大喊，卻發不出聲音。他的本能比他的意識更早地認出了正在發生的事情。弗朗西斯仍然站在那裡，但他正在變成一棵樹。他的雙腳陷進泥土，根鬚正在試探著向下伸展。他的肩胛骨頂起了工裝布的背面，那兩個凸起的弧度正在變寬，正在和周圍的氣生根、藤蔓、附生蘭花的根系交織在一起，像一隻正在織網的蜘蛛把自己紡進了網的中心。
On the black-water river of the Amazon, an arapaima, diving, scraped its chest against a submerged stone and left a scale. That scale later became ore; the ore was carried away by tree roots; the tree was pecked by a toucan's beak; a butterfly landed on the beak. That scale, that ore, that tree, and that butterfly shared the most ancient language. The gaze as he crossed the rainforest—the rainforest itself was gazing through his eyes, touching through his fingers, growing through his silence. 亞馬遜的黑水河上，一條巨骨舌魚在下潛時，把胸膛剮在了一塊水下的石頭上，留下了一片鱗。那片鱗後來成了礦石，礦石被樹根捲走，樹根被巨嘴鳥鳥啄進了喙裡，喙上落了一隻蝴蝶。那片鱗、那塊礦石、那棵樹和那隻蝴蝶共同的、最古老的語言。他穿過雨林時的那種注視，雨林本身在透過他的眼睛注視，透過他的手指觸摸，透過他的沉默生長。
Arthur's legs gave way; his knees sank into the leaf litter. His eyes were still open, but his vision began to blur, as if an extremely thin, watery film were growing from his field of vision. He saw—those gazes. They had always been there, on the back of every leaf, in every drop of falling sap, in the faint glow at the tip of every butterfly's wing. Thousands of eyes pressed against his back, and now they were seeping out of his vision, spreading into a chaotic light. His breathing became the sound of tearing. 亞瑟的腿軟了一下，膝蓋陷進腐葉層裡。他的眼睛還睜著，但視線開始模糊，像有一層極薄的、水汽般的膜正從他的視野裡長出來。他看見了——那些注視。它們一直在那裡，在每一片葉子的背面，在每一滴垂落的樹汁裡，在每一隻蝴蝶翅尖的微光裡。千萬雙眼睛貼在他的背上，此刻正從他的視野裡滲出來，瀰漫成一片混沌的光。他的呼吸變成了被撕開的聲響。

The clouds pressed low; the rain threads were like countless fine stitches, sewing sky and ground together. The logging machine lay scattered around; no one was in sight. The canopy reached into the sky; the rain-wet leaves gave off a dark green lustre, their edges curled like the rims of porcelain bowls, water droplets rolling down the curled edges and dripping onto Arthur's forehead. The feedback state of all my articles, especially "The Weather Is Clear Far Away," has left me deeply disappointed. One afternoon, a week after publication, I decided on the next piece from my existing outlines. 我所有文章，尤其是《遠方天氣晴朗》的反饋狀態讓我非常失望。發布一週後的一個下午，我從已有的大綱中決定下一篇文章。
In the past, many of my pieces had images existing before words, so they were written very smoothly. That afternoon, although I had the desire to create, not a single image was born in my mind. To be honest, I have always been able to distinguish my creative impulses—including then, part of it came from "I must write a piece to prove myself again," and another part came from "I am hoping the feedback on the new piece will reduce the coldness and pain that 'The Weather Is Clear Far Away' brought me." I am only admitting this and still choosing to force myself. But in reality, for a creator like me, a new piece is also very likely to remain without feedback. 過去我有很多篇目是畫面先於文字存在，因此寫得十分順暢。那個下午雖然有創作欲，卻沒有一篇在我腦中生出畫面。說實話，我一直能分辨我的創作衝動——包括那時，一部分來自「我一定要寫一篇重新證明自己」，另一部分則來自「我在期待新篇的反饋能削減《遠方天氣晴朗》帶給我的冷淡與痛苦」。我只是在承認這點之後依舊會選擇逼迫自己。但實際上，對於我這樣的創作者來說，新篇也很可能依然毫無反饋。
However, apart from those relatively complete outline subjects, some scattered images were born in my mind—a botanist rowing a boat into the depths of the Amazon rainforest. The earliest source of this image should be my favourite animated film, Rio. In the stuffy air before rain, in my bedroom, I saw damp, sweating bodies, giant trees, rivers, air… and an indescribable feeling: 但是，除了那些相對完整的大綱題材之外，我腦子裡生出一些零星的畫面——一個植物學家划船進入亞馬遜雨林深處。這個畫面最早的源頭應該是我最喜歡的動畫電影《里約大冒險》。在雨前悶熱的空氣中，我的臥室裡，我看見潮濕流汗的身體、巨大的樹木、河流、空氣……以及一種難以言說的感覺：
"There are so many living things that you will always feel someone is watching your back." 「生靈太多，以至於你會永遠覺得有人在注視你的背影。」
This gaze does not necessarily mean a terrifying presence, but is more like an existence where all things have spirits. Everything is looking at you; the world is alive. This gaze is not coercion either; it is telling you that you are not alone. When the density of life is sufficiently high—for example, in a rainforest—loneliness itself is impossible. You do not need to believe in gods; you only need to be in a sufficiently lush forest, and you will passively enter a state of "being seen"—not seen by some虚无 subject, but seen by the density of existence itself. 這種注視未必意味著恐怖的存在，而是更像一種萬物皆有靈的存在感。一切都在看你，世界是活著的。這種注視也不是脅迫，它是在告訴你你並不孤獨。當生命密度足夠大時——比如在雨林，孤獨本身就是不可能的。你不需要相信神明，只要置身於足夠茂盛的森林，你就會被動地進入一種「被看見」的狀態——不是被某個虛無的主體看見，而是被存在的密度本身看見。
In this somewhat nostalgic creation, this piece was completed. Although, due to severe tenosynovitis and migraines, it was much later than I had expected. 在這種有點像懷念的創作中，這篇完成了。儘管因為嚴重的腱鞘炎與偏頭痛，比我預想的晚了很多。
At first I wanted to write the Holy Spirit as some kind of personified existence. Later I gradually discovered that he might not be a person at all; he is the collection of life. It is just that he appears in the form Arthur likes most, is most familiar with, and can best understand, even if Arthur himself does not admit it. Because the Holy Spirit is not tempting him with a form he likes, but translating itself. Trees, rivers, birds do not understand human language, so the entire forest can only use the most vivid, most acceptable image in the botanist's senses to communicate with him. I made Francis silent, first because I feel that in many articles, including the original work, Francis is a person who relies heavily on language to flirt. I wanted to try what remains if you take away a character's most proficient means of expression. Many characters are charming precisely because they can talk, and can establish intimacy most directly with sweet words. But if all that is gone, his charm can only come from his non-verbal ways of interacting with others. Second, he himself is composed of tens of thousands of sounds. Compared to language—a system to which humans assign meaning—sound is the original existence of life. The rainforest and the streets are not speaking Portuguese, but they are all making sound. And these sounds share a common rhythm, and this rhythm is also connected to human language. Only then does the repeatedly appearing "Aiú" hold. It is a syllable that all life can emit. It can be a baby's first vowel, the wind's friction through tree crowns, the elongated tail note in birdsong, a person's last breath at death, the echo of river water striking stones… It does not need translation at all; it already existed before becoming language. Moreover, language creates fixed attributes. Just like if you speak French, you belong to France; if you speak Chinese, you belong to China. (Don't pointlessly argue with me here that African colonies also speak French, and Singapore also speaks Chinese. You know I don't mean that.) But Francis does not belong to any country; he belongs to life. So the best way is to let him not enter any language at all, retaining only sound, gaze, movement, touch, breathing… these expressions shared by all life. 最初我想把聖靈寫成某種人格化的存在。後來慢慢發現他或許根本不是一個人，他是生命的集合。只是他會以亞瑟最喜歡、最熟悉、最能理解的樣貌出現，哪怕亞瑟自己都不承認。因為聖靈並非在以他喜歡的樣貌誘惑他，而是在翻譯自己。樹木、河流、鳥兒都不懂人的語言，於是整片森林只能借用植物學家感官裡最鮮活的、最能接受的形象與他交流。我把弗朗西斯設定成不說話，一是因為我覺得很多文章，包括原作裡，弗朗西斯都是一個非常依賴語言進行flirt的人。我想試試看如果把一個角色最擅長的表達方式拿走，他還剩下什麼？很多角色之所以有魅力，就是因為他們會說話，可以用情話最直觀地建立親密。但如果這些都沒有了，他的魅力只能來自他與別人進行非語言互動的方式。二是他本身而是由千萬種聲音組成。比起語言——人類賦予意義的系統。聲音是生命本來的存在。雨林和街道不是在說葡語，可是它們都在發聲。而這些聲音之間會有共同的節律，且這個節律也是與人的語言相通的。這樣文中反覆出現的「Aiú」才成立。它是一個所有生命都可能發出的音節。它可以是嬰兒學會的第一個元音，風穿過樹冠的摩擦，鳥鳴裡拉長的尾音，人臨終時最後一口呼吸，河水撞擊石頭的回響……它根本不需要翻譯，在成為語言之前就已經存在。況且語言會製造固有屬性。就好像你说法語，你就屬於法國，你說中文，你就屬於中國。（別在這裡毫無意義地槓我非洲殖民區也说法語，以及新加坡也說中文。你知道我不是這個意思。）但是弗朗西斯不屬於任何一個國家，而是屬於生命。那麼最好的辦法就是讓他根本不進入任何一種語言，只保留聲音、眼神、動作、觸碰、呼吸……這些所有生命都共有的表達。
I once wanted to write the ending as "the anger of life or nature," but this expression gradually lost accuracy in the process of thinking. Anger is an overly anthropomorphic human emotion. My hometown is in the mountainous area of southern Anhui, where there are large tracts of tall moso bamboo forests. The growth of bamboo is rapid and savage; one root will produce countless shoots. If you find a bamboo shoot emerging from your floor, then the next day the entire floor will likely be pierced by countless shoots. But this is not because nature is punishing you, but because nature is doing what it should do—growing freely—and you just happen to be in its path. Nature never takes revenge; life simply continues to grow. 我一度想把結尾寫成「生命或自然的憤怒」，但這種表達在思考過程中逐漸失準。憤怒是一種過度擬人化的人類情緒。我老家在皖南山區，那裡有大片高大的毛竹林。竹子的生長是迅速而野蠻的，一條根上會長出無數筍尖。如果你發現家裡的地板上冒出一根筍頭，那麼第二天很可能整塊地板都會被無數根筍刺穿。但這不是因為大自然在懲罰你，而是大自然做了它應該做的事情——自由地生長，而你恰好就在它的路徑上。自然從不復仇，生命只是繼續生長。
Beyond the depiction of the Amazon rainforest, I also added depictions of human culture. If the Holy Spirit is truly composed of the collection of life, then he should not only come from the forest. He should also come from carnival drumbeats, street graffiti, fruits in the market, children's laughter, fishing boats by the river… from humans, because humans are always part of life. The rainforest is his roots, the coast is his breathing, samba is his heartbeat, the crowd is his other kind of canopy. 在對亞馬遜雨林的描寫之外，我也加入了對人文的描寫。聖靈如果真的由生命集合構成，那麼他不應該只來自森林。他也應該來自狂歡節鼓點、街頭塗鴉、市場裡的水果、孩子的笑聲、河邊漁船……來自人類，因為人永遠是生命的一部分。雨林是他的根，海岸是他的呼吸，桑巴是他的心跳，人群是他的另一種樹冠。
At the same time, I also began to think about why I have been so长久地 fascinated by Brazil. My father is a Brazil fan, and I myself am a Brazil fan and a loyal viewer of football matches. Four years after four years, another World Cup year. Unlike recent World Cup themes or promotional songs that pursue a certain musical innovation, listening to older World Cup theme songs, you seem to clearly hear the spiritual outlook and character of each country in its melody. The first line of the 2010 South Africa World Cup was "You are a good soldier"—fitting perfectly with the primitive, vigorous fighting instinct of African countries, related to the grasslands, long independence struggles, and repeated explorations of war and chaos. The 2014 Brazil theme song was "We Are One." "Let the world know we are one." Of course I know that in reality Brazil has enormous wealth gaps, crime rates, and social problems. But the Brazil of that era showed the world another spiritual picture: not competition, but coexistence. Not homogenisation, but fusion. This emotion has actually continued into my creation. Why is the Holy Spirit the collection of life? Why carnival, rainforest ecology? Because they all express the same theme. Speaking of Rio and the 2014 Brazil World Cup, many people think the influence of childhood is just nostalgia, but I don't think so. Some works enter a person's life at a particularly delicate age, and then quietly determine what you will be moved by later. What I loved as a child may not have shaped my views, but it often shaped my sensibility—determining what I would find beautiful, what I would yearn for. Why would Brazil's Holy Spirit be the "collection of life"? Perhaps not because I have studied animism, nor because I deliberately designed a philosophical meaning, but because since 2014, I have already been moved by an imagination—that differences in the world do not necessarily mean rupture; they may also mean coexistence. 同時，我也開始思考我為什麼會對巴西如此長久地著迷。我爸爸是巴西球迷，我自己也是巴西球迷與球賽的忠實觀眾。四年又四年，又是一年世界盃。與近幾年世界盃主題或宣傳曲開始追求一定的音樂創新性不同，聽以前的世界盃主題曲，你似乎能從每個國家的旋律裡清晰地聽見那個國家的精神面貌與性格。2010年南非世界盃的第一句是「你是個優秀的戰士」——與非洲國家那種與草原、長久的獨立抗爭與反覆探索的戰亂息息相關的，原始而旺盛的戰鬥本能無比契合。而2014年巴西的主題曲是《We Are One》。「讓世界知道我們是一家。」我當然知道現實中巴西存在巨大的貧富差距、犯罪率和社會問題。但那個時代的巴西向世界展現的是另一種精神圖景：不是競爭，而是共存。不是同質化，而是融合。這種情感其實一直延續到了我的創作裡。為什麼聖靈是生命的集合？為什麼是狂歡節、雨林生態？因為它們都在表達同一個主題。說回《里約大冒險》與2014巴西世界盃，很多人會以為童年的影響只是懷舊，但我覺得不是。有些作品會在一個特別微妙的年齡進入人的生命，然後悄悄決定你以後會被什麼打動。我小時候喜歡的東西未必塑造了我的觀點，但往往塑造了我的感受力——決定了我會覺得什麼是美，什麼值得嚮往。巴西的聖靈為什麼會是「生命集合」？可能不是因為我研究過泛靈論，也不是刻意設計哲學寓意，而是因為從2014年開始，我就已經被一種想象打動了——世界上的差異不一定意味著撕裂，它們也可能意味著共存。
I also want to use a stereotypically Brazilian spirit to express my attitude toward "pain." I am not saying these words to people who truly suffer; I understand those who truly experience illness, trauma, and despair. But I always remain vigilant against another phenomenon—treating world-weariness as a label, destruction as personality, packaging pain as profundity. "The more desperate I am, the more special I am." Desire and violence can of course become literary themes. But if a person can ultimately only prove their uniqueness through a sense of destruction, then this uniqueness itself is fragile. Rivers do not stop flowing because a person is desperate; trees do not stop growing because a person feels the world is meaningless. Life never stops to wait for us or to solve existential problems for us; it simply continues to exist. Admiration is actually a severely underrated creative motivation. Many authors write anger, sorrow, fear. But those who truly write out of admiration are few. In this piece, I no longer ask what social significance the Amazon has. When I see that land, I am moved; this being moved is closer to the source of creation than any data analysis. Don't think that world-weariness and pain make you different; in fact, we are all the same. Under the scale of nature, personality is constantly dissolved, reorganised, defined, and redefined. What is truly worthy of awe is not pain, but life itself. And the essence of life is precisely continuity that is not obstructed by emotion. Life does not deny darkness, but darkness is not the endpoint, nor even the protagonist. You do not need to first solve absurdity before you can begin to flow. The most moving thing about Rio, which I loved most as a child, was never what profound truths it told, but that it made one feel how vivid the world is, how vigorous life is. And to be able to be within it is itself something worthy of awe. 我還想借一種刻板上的巴西精神表達自己對「痛苦」的態度。我並非對真正遭受痛苦的人說這些話，我理解那些真正經歷疾病、創傷和絕境的人。但我始終對另一種現象保持警惕——把厭世當作標籤，把毀滅當作個性，把痛苦包裝成深刻。「我越絕望，我就越特別」。慾望與暴力當然可以成為文學主題。但如果一個人最終只能通過毀滅感來證明自己的獨特，那麼這種獨特本身就是脆弱的。河流不會因為一個人絕望而停止流動，樹木不會因為一個人覺得世界沒有意義而停止生長。生命永遠不會停下來等待我們、或者為我們解決存在主義問題，它只是繼續存在。讚嘆其實是一種被嚴重低估的創作動力。很多作者會寫憤怒、悲傷、恐懼。但真正因為讚嘆而寫作的人很少。我在這篇中不再問亞馬遜有什麼社會意義。我看到那片土地的時候會感動，這種感動比任何數據分析都更接近創作的源頭。不要覺得厭世和痛苦就與眾不同，實際上我們都是一樣的。在自然的尺度下，人格會被不斷消弭、重組、定義、再定義。真正值得敬畏的不是痛苦，而是生命本身。而生命的本質恰恰是不受情緒阻礙的延續性。生命不否認黑暗，但黑暗不是終點，甚至不是主角。你不必先解決荒誕再開始流動。我年幼時最愛的《里約大冒險》最動人的地方從來不是它講了多麼深刻的道理，而是它讓人感受到世界如此鮮豔，生命如此旺盛。而能夠身處其中，本身就是一件值得驚嘆的事。
Of course, this idea can easily be misunderstood as belittling human existence. I have repeatedly examined this point, and the final answer I reached is no. I am not negating humans, nor am I praising some grand whole that swallows the individual. What I want to express is that humans are not the only scale, but humans are still one of the scales. I do not think individuals should melt into the whole. On the contrary, I have always believed that even if you are a part, two leaves of the same species have different textures. If there were no these concrete, microscopic lives—no individual experience and feeling—then any macro narrative would ultimately become an empty conjecture.
