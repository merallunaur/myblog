The Weather Is Clear Far Away 遠方天氣晴朗

◇
Francis's breath landed on my shoulder. I kept pressing the accelerator, driving into the midnight sun. A few days ago, we had landed in Helsinki, walking out of the cabin hand in hand under the dazzling sunlight. The dryness before midsummer instantly stripped away the damp, warm air clinging to my body, like pulling off a sweater soaked with water. The sea wind flowed through our sweating fingers, like putting a hand through lake water. 弗朗西斯的呼吸在我肩膀上降落，我不斷加大油門，驅車駛入極晝。幾日前，我們降落在赫爾辛基，在耀眼的日光下牽手走出機艙。仲夏前的乾燥頃刻將我身上潮濕溫熱的空氣脫去，如同脫去一件吸飽水的毛衣。海風流過我們流著汗的十指，像把手穿過湖水。
Where did the memory of lake water come from? I had never swum. Being bare-chested in front of other children always made me unbearably shy. In the season when the sun was fiercest, I hid indoors. The laughter from the pool in the distance pounded against my chest. Never having been exposed to the sun, my skin was as white as moonlight. After I gradually grew into self-awareness, Francis was the first person who had ever seen me naked. He always brushed his burning fingertips over my thin shoulder blades before kissing me, over ribs like a row of piano keys and sharp knees. This was a body forever unrelated to beauty. Sometimes I felt he was more of an artist than I was. 關於湖水的記憶來自哪裡？我從未游過泳，在其他孩子面前赤裸上身永遠使我羞怯難當。在太陽最烈的季節，我都躲在屋子裡。遠處水池邊的笑聲砸向我的胸膛，沒有經過日曬，我的皮膚像月光一樣白。在我逐步成長出自我意識後，弗朗西斯是第一個見過我裸體的人。他總是在親吻之前用滾燙的指腹掠過我瘦削的肩胛，如一排琴鍵似的肋骨與尖尖的膝蓋。這是一具永遠與美無關的身體。有時候，我覺得他比我更像一位藝術家。
I passed through the takeoff and landing wind that smelled of garbage. The wind poured into my throat; I felt like I was biting into a rotting apple. Wildflowers were blooming beneath the silver-grey walls of the airport. Here, everything was timed by fragrance. When we arrived in the morning, the air smelled of lily of the valley wet with dew. The sun placed its frenzied heat on my forehead. The scent of wild roses, carrying a hint of honey and myrrh, filled my restless chest. In the evening we checked into an inn. A bouquet of lilacs stood in the vase on the table. I took one sprig and pressed it into my sketchbook. This was the first souvenir I took away. I am not good at farewells. The journey had only just begun, and I had already begun rehearsing the goodbye. We had slept for a long time on the plane, so we could only lie awake in the hotel waiting for the next fragrance. The trial that had just ended had left Francis exhausted; even after getting off the plane, he remained taciturn. He had defended a man accused of kidnapping and killing a little girl. The body had been burned. DNA, surveillance footage, search records—all the evidence pointed to him. The media called him a "monster." Like many murderers, the man's face was utterly ordinary—and that was exactly what Francis needed. He did not deny any of the evidence. Instead, he made the man human again. A depressed but habitually kind middle-aged man. A man whose marriage had failed. A man who ate the same sandwich for lunch every day, who didn't even dare to argue with his wife. He called psychologists, divorce lawyers, colleagues. In the end, he persuaded the jury. This was not a perfect murderer. This was a broken ordinary person who, under the side effects of medication, had developed intrusive thoughts. This kind of person could be found everywhere around us. He won. The man was convicted of manslaughter and faced twelve years in prison. First-degree murder and kidnapping did not hold. And the girl had not been twelve years old. That night, he came home with two bottles of wine and lazily rested his head on my shoulder. 我從散發著垃圾氣味的飛機起落風中穿過。風灌入喉嚨，我像咬著一口腐爛的蘋果。機場銀灰色的圍牆下野花正怒放。這裡的一切由香氣計時。早上我們到達時，空氣聞起來像沾著露水的鈴蘭。太陽把癲狂的燥熱放在我額頭上，帶著一點蜂蜜與沒藥氣息的野玫瑰香氣瀰漫在我心跳不止的胸膛中。傍晚我們入住旅社，桌子上的花瓶中插著一束丁香。我拿走一枝夾在我的速寫本裡。這是我帶走的第一個紀念品。我不擅長告別。旅程剛剛開始，我也同樣開始為告別做預演。我們在飛機上睡了很久，只能在旅館睜著眼睛等待下一陣香氣。剛結束的辯護讓弗朗西斯疲憊不堪，他下了飛機依舊少言寡語。他為一個被指控綁架並殺死了一個女童的男人辯護。屍體被焚燒過。DNA、監控、搜索記錄，所有的證據都指向他。媒體稱他為「怪物」。與很多殺人犯一樣，男人的臉普通得不能再普通——而這正是弗朗西斯所需要的。他沒有否認任何證據，而是讓男人做回一個普通人。一個抑鬱但習慣善良的中年人。一個婚姻失敗的人。一個每天吃同樣的三明治當午餐，連和妻子吵架都不敢的人。他傳喚了心理醫生、離婚律師、同事。他最終說服了陪審團。這不是一個完美的謀殺者，這是一個破碎的、在藥物副作用下產生了侵入性思想的普通人。這個人在我們身邊隨處可見。他勝利了，男人被判過失殺人，面臨十二年監禁。一級謀殺和綁架不成立。而那個女孩沒有十二歲。那天晚上，他拿著兩瓶酒回家，懶洋洋地把頭擱在我的肩膀上。
"I should be used to it, Arthur." 「我應該習慣了，亞瑟。」
I was also going through a period of creative drought. Whether to leave or to re-enter life, we decided to leave home and clear our heads. In Helsinki during the day, we only ordered a bowl of cream of salmon soup; the peculiar saltiness killed our appetites. It was only when the hotel sent up cinnamon rolls that Francis managed to eat a little. 我一樣正經歷靈感枯竭的時期。無論為了離開，或是重新進入生活，我們決定離開家散心。在赫爾辛基的白天我們只點了一碗奶油三文魚湯，特殊的鹹味讓我們沒什麼胃口。直到酒店送來肉桂捲時，弗朗西斯才勉強吃了一點。
We did not stay long in Helsinki. The next day we took the night train toward Rovaniemi.① I woke once at two in the morning; outside the window, the sky was still the blue of denim. The forest grew denser. I could not sleep, so I lay there drawing the blurry scene outside. The air conditioning had turned off at some point; sweat ran down my back. I sat up and silently counted the quiet time before the announcement. I could not understand the staccato murmur of Finnish, like an elf whispering spells in my ear. As with countless futile attempts before, I tried to construct the structure of the picture from scratch, but my mind was blank. I lay back down and turned my face toward Francis's berth. At some station along the way, an old man wearing antler ornaments had boarded. He was now sitting on Francis's bed, saying nothing. I whispered at him to leave. He raised his yellowed, bulging eyes and looked at me uncomprehendingly. We could not understand each other's language, so we each turned our heads away. Francis did not wake until we arrived. His breathing when asleep was very light; I often had to turn my head to confirm he was there. 我們沒在赫爾辛基待太久，第二天就坐夜火車向羅瓦涅米①進發。凌晨兩點我醒來一次，窗外的天依舊是牛仔褲一樣的藍色。森林越來越密集，我睡不著，躺著畫窗外模糊的景象。空調不知什麼時候關閉了，汗水流過我的背。我坐起身，默默倒數著廣播響起前的安靜時間。我聽不懂芬蘭語頓挫的低語，就像精靈在我耳邊訴說咒語。和之前的無數次徒勞一樣，我試圖從頭構建起畫面的結構，但腦中只有一片空白。我又躺了回去，將臉轉向弗朗西斯的床鋪。中途的某站上來一個戴著鹿角飾品的老人，此時他正坐在弗朗西斯的床上，一言不發。我小聲驅趕他，他抬起昏黃突出的眼球，一臉不解地望著我。我們互相聽不懂彼此的語言，只能各自把頭轉回去。到站前，弗朗西斯都沒有醒。他睡著時的呼吸很輕，我經常要轉頭確認他的存在。
Reindeer stew had always baffled me. We sat in the centre of the restaurant. A fan gave off a gasoline smell, rotating cautiously. Like stray animals by the roadside cautiously approaching the road, drinking from a puddle covered with an oil film. Francis leaned back against a large middle-aged man, so he could only lean forward, nose to nose with me. The gentleman was clearly one of the staff who played Santa Claus, yet he was speaking Russian into the phone in a rough voice. The fan blew his long white beard, which then fell into the sauce on the reindeer meat, like a layer of dried white mould floating on the soup. 馴鹿燉肉這道菜一直讓我匪夷所思。我們坐在餐館的中心位置，風扇散發著一股汽油味，小心翼翼地旋轉。就像路邊的流浪動物謹慎地靠近馬路，從浮著油膜的水坑裡飲水。弗朗西斯背靠一位體格龐大的中年男人，因此只能向前趴，鼻子對著我的鼻子。那位先生顯然是扮演聖誕老人的工作人員之一，他卻對著電話，用粗獷的嗓音說俄語。風扇吹起他長長的白鬍鬚，又落在馴鹿肉上的醬汁裡，像湯裡飄著一層乾枯的白黴。
"Don't you think it's strange? Santa eating his own mount." 「你不覺得奇怪嗎？聖誕老人吃自己的坐騎。」
Thank God, the posture gave Francis great convenience. He leaned forward completely inconspicuously and whispered in my ear. I couldn't help but laugh out loud at the association. The gentleman turned and glanced at me. I had to bury my head and continue cutting the reindeer meat. 謝天謝地，姿勢給了弗朗西斯極大的便利。他完全不引人注目地往前湊了湊，輕聲在我耳邊說。我實在忍不住被這種聯想逗得笑出聲，那位先生回頭看了我一眼。我只好又埋頭切起馴鹿肉。
"Children mustn't know about this." 「小孩子可不能知道這些。」
Francis added. I laughed again. More people looked at me. I hurriedly paid the bill and backed out of the restaurant. 弗朗西斯又補上一句。我又笑了。更多人看向我。我匆忙結賬，倒退著離開餐廳。
Although it was not Christmas season, various Santa Claus decorations still hung from the trees in Rovaniemi. Large areas of red tangled with the emerald green of the forest, tiring my senses. I pressed my index finger and thumb against my brow to relieve the soreness in my eyes. Francis suggested we count how many Santa decorations there were along the way. Every few seconds he would call out a number. When he reached one hundred and five, he suggested we go to a souvenir shop and buy a commemorative coin, to decide who would drive to the final destination. The Santa engraved on one side smiled as usual. He handed it to me without opening the package, saying he would choose the reverse. His eyes trembled as he spoke; the dark circles under his eyes swayed like a lily pad. I looked at his eyes and frowned. I tossed the coin and pressed it onto the back of my hand. 儘管不是聖誕季，羅瓦涅米的樹上依舊掛著各式各樣的聖誕老人。大面積的紅色與森林的翠綠纏在一起，使我感官疲勞。我用食指與拇指按住眉心，緩解眼睛的酸痛。弗朗西斯則提議我們數一數沿途有多少個聖誕老人裝飾。他每隔幾秒就要念個數字。當數到第一百零五個時，他又提議我們去紀念品店買一枚紀念幣，以此決定誰來開向最終目的地。硬幣單面刻著的聖誕老人像往常一樣笑容可掬。他沒拆開包裝就遞給了我，說自己選反面。他說話時顫抖著雙眼，眼下的烏青如一片睡蓮葉似的晃動。我看著他的眼睛，皺了皺眉。拋起硬幣按在我的手背上。
"It's heads. I'll drive." 「是正面，我來開。」
"Oh? Oh... alright then. If you get too tired, you must wake me." 「哦？哦……那好吧，如果太累了，一定要叫醒我。」
We walked out of the restaurant that smelled of game and grease and got into the rented car. He had already predicted he would sleep like this. At first he forced himself to stay awake and talk to me, but after a while his breathing became soft. When we passed Lake Inari,② we hit a bump. The car groaned a few times and then stopped completely. I was a little scared. I patted his shoulder, but he felt nothing. I could not wake him. 我們走出散發著膻味和油脂氣息的餐館，鑽進租來的小車裡。他已經預料到自己會如此刻一般沉睡，一開始強撐著精神跟我說話，但過了一會兒呼吸就變得輕柔。我們路過伊納里湖②時經歷了一陣顛簸，車呻吟了幾句，徹底不動了。我有些害怕，拍拍他的肩膀，但他卻什麼也沒感覺到。我沒能把他叫醒。
Legends of bandits were widespread in this area. Bandits would place obstacles on the road, puncture travellers' cars, and then, when the travellers got out, rush out from the dense forest to rob them—a proper old Viking style. Nevertheless, I got out of the car alone. There were a few metres of drag marks behind the rear wheel. I bent down, supporting myself on the rear hood, and shone a flashlight inside. A severed deer leg had somehow become lodged in the wheel. I had no memory of hitting anything. I swept it out from deep inside with a bat. The stump bore the tooth marks of a wild animal; the blood was still bright red. 這一帶廣為流傳強盜的傳說。強盜會在路上放障礙，扎壞旅客的車之後，趁旅客下車就從密林衝出來搶奪錢財，一副老維京人做派。儘管如此，我還是獨自下車。後輪有幾米的拖行血跡。我扶著車後蓋彎下腰，用手電筒照著裡面。車輪裡不知什麼時候卡著一截鹿的斷肢。我沒有撞到過任何東西的記憶，用球棒將它從深處掃出來。斷口有野獸的齒痕，血依舊鮮紅。
I had never experienced the death of any living thing, and the smell of blood made my head swim. Yet I still felt I had an obligation to bury this severed limb. I wrapped it loosely in a handkerchief, lifted the still-warm limb, and walked toward the lake. As I placed the limb down, my fingers reached into the lake water. The limb sank immediately, carried away by the undercurrent. But in my mind, I smelled the scent of a swimming pool, the smell of chlorine. My ears were full of laughter. The cool lake water lured me. I took off my shoes, my socks stepping on the stones of the shore. The water passed my ankles, then my knees, finally my thighs. The lake water was cold, unlike June. 我沒有經歷過任何活物的死亡，血的气味也讓我頭腦昏沉，我卻依舊覺得自己對這根斷肢負有安葬的義務。我用手帕稍稍包裹著，抱起這根毛皮尚且溫熱的斷肢，走向湖邊。我的手指在安放斷肢的同時伸進湖水。斷肢馬上沉了下去，隨著湖底暗流被捲走。我的腦中卻聞到了泳池的氣味、氯的氣味。我的耳中都是歡笑。清涼的湖水引誘著我。我脫了鞋，襪子踩在湖岸的石頭上。水沒過腳踝，接著是膝蓋，最後是大腿。湖水冷得不像六月。
"Cool?" 「涼快嗎？」
A light, cold breath suddenly came to my ear. Francis's arm around my neck was like a thick noose. Since he entered law school that year, he had kept up his fitness routine to this day. Sometimes there were emergencies—this was a reason I knew well. The people he defended were always a group of vicious murderers. But he could never entirely abandon his sensibility, like being unable to peel off a layer of skin, or losing his heart. His sensibility was a warm, bloody thing, something that needed to be hidden beneath the iron blade of rules. He had always done it well, so well that it seemed he had never possessed it. But sometimes, when I looked into his eyes during the hangovers after each trial, I still believed that the blindfolded woman's raised scales could not bear his anger, which was equal to his guilt. 我的耳邊突然傳來又輕又冷的呼吸，弗朗西斯環繞我脖子的胳膊如一圈粗絞索。自從那年入學法學院後，他便將健身習慣保持至今。有時會有突發情況——這是我熟知的理由。他的辯護對象永遠是一群窮凶惡極的殺人犯。但他卻永遠無法全然拋棄感性，就像剝不下一層皮膚，抑或是失去心臟。他所擅長的感性是一件帶有溫度的，血肉模糊的東西，需要在規則的鐵銬下隱藏的東西。他一直做得很好，好到彷彿從未擁有過。但有時候，當我在每個庭審之後的宿醉中望向他的眼睛。我依舊認為那架被蒙眼的女人高舉的天平，無法承受與罪惡相等的他的憤怒。
"Mm." 「嗯。」
I gripped his hand on my shoulder tightly, like a lookout sailor gripping the mast. The lake rippled endlessly; waterweed tangled around my ankles. 我緊攥住他放在我肩上的手，像一個瞭望的水手緊抓住桅杆。湖波搖曳不止，水草纏住我的腳踝。
"It's dangerous." 「很危險。」
"Then pull me up quickly. No nonsense." 「那就趕緊拉我上去，不要廢話。」
He smiled almost imperceptibly and placed his other hand on my waist. Sunlight was like dozens of iron needles piercing through the leaves. My gaze crossed the treetops, trying to piece together some image of the sunset. But there was nothing. Everything was as white as a newly made robe. The night was bright; the sky was always light. I started the car and got back on the road, looking at Francis. At some point he had fallen asleep again. 他微不可察地笑了一下，將另一隻手放在我的腰上。太陽光如幾十根穿越樹葉的鐵針。我的目光越過樹冠，試圖拼湊起有關晚霞的一些圖景。但是什麼都沒有，一切都如新做的長袍一般潔白。夜晚是光明的，天永遠亮堂。我發動汽車重新上路，望向弗朗西斯。不知什麼時候他又睡著了。

The village. 村莊。
I chose the destination by intuition. Sometimes I would write names of countries on cards, throw them into the air, and let that decide where I should go. This time, however, I was drawn directly to the website of a Finnish village. Its information came from a sidebar advertisement on a travel forum, suddenly discovered after a long dormancy. As if you only realise, when you decide to clean your room, that thirty rings of spiderwebs have already formed on the ceiling. The unknown distance has always captivated me. I long for the distance like a person without a homeland. I clicked twice; under a sky whose colour was unclear, whether from fading or from the midnight sun, stood a reindeer. A person in a dark blue robe straddled the deer's back, holding up a drum like a child held aloft. The low resolution made it impossible for me to see the wind-eroded characters on the drum's surface. But the reindeer's eyes were a translucent pale colour, as if sunk into the eternal daylight sky. It looked at the camera with a gaze that almost did not belong to an animal. Yet, apart from this strange person and beast, the webpage's title was in several completely outdated sapphire art fonts, like a string of meaningless hippie street graffiti. I couldn't help but sigh. 我用直覺確定旅行地。有時我會在卡片上寫上一些國家的名字，將他們拋向空中，以此確定自己該去往何處。這次我卻直接被這個芬蘭村莊的網站吸引。它的信息來自一個旅行論壇的邊欄廣告，在長久的蟄伏中突然被發現。彷彿你決定打掃房間時，才意識到天花板上已經結了三十圈的蜘蛛網。未知的遠方一向使我心馳神往。我渴求遠方，就像一個沒有故鄉的人。我點了兩下鼠標，在不知是因為褪色還是極晝的天空下站著一隻馴鹿。一個穿深藍色長袍的人跨在鹿背，像高舉著嬰孩那樣高舉一面鼓。低像素使我看不清鼓面上風蝕過的文字。但馴鹿的眼睛是通透的淺色，如同陷入永晝的天空。它用一種幾乎不屬於動物的眼神看著鏡頭。然而，脫離近乎奇異的人與獸，網頁的標題竟是幾個完全過時的寶藍色藝術字，彷彿一串毫無內容的嬉皮士街頭塗鴉。我忍不住嘆氣。
"The Land of Daylight, where the soul sees its animal." 「白晝之地，靈魂看見它的動物。」
Below the page were tourist photos. Different masked people stood before the same fire pit, with grey grassland and sky behind them. They were smiling. Arms around each other's shoulders, bowls in their hands, or crouching beside a reindeer making victory signs. The wreaths around their necks were like nooses. I leaned back in my chair; the pencil tucked behind my ear was about to fall. "Mm." 「嗯。」

I did not argue. He closed the tab and pushed the coffee cup toward me. This was the entire reason we stood before the village. This year, we were the only tourists. Endless grassland, giving off a strong fragrance, with wild roses and lilies of the valley blooming. 我沒有反駁。他關掉了標籤頁，把咖啡杯推到我面前。這就是我們站在村莊前的全部原因。今年的遊客只有我們。一個強壯的村人幫我們把車還了回去。站在山坡上，我瞭望四周。一望無際，散發著濃烈香味的草原上野玫瑰和鈴蘭綻放。

Something in his dream touched him lightly; his eyelashes and eyelids trembled faintly. I placed my hand on his forehead and breathed deeply and slowly. His brow gradually relaxed, and his breathing frequency matched mine, like two facing pages in a book. I looked up. The sun was probably in some direction, but I could not tell. Since arriving, I had never felt the warmth of summer. 香氣四溢的清晨，我側過頭看弗朗西斯垂落在枕上的淺金色長髮。在灰色光線裡，他幾乎要融進亞麻布的顏色裡去。有什麼東西在夢裡輕輕碰了碰他，他的睫毛與眼皮輕輕顫抖。我將手放在他的額頭上，又深又快地呼吸。他的眉頭逐漸舒展開，呼吸頻率也與我一致，像一本書中挨著的兩頁紙。我抬頭，太陽大概在某個方向，但我無法辨別。到來之後，我還從未感到過來自夏天的溫暖。

More often, drawing was how I fought against farewells. I looked at Francis's face for so long that sometimes I no longer recognised it. Then I would close my eyes and reconstruct him starting from his voice. His brow bone was high, the ends of his eyebrows slightly drooping, making him look as if he were always pondering a thorny matter. His lips only changed shape when he smiled. The shape of the auricle was like a shell folding its wings, with a small hollow on the earlobe. I had noticed this the first time I saw him. His ear piercing was right beside this hollow. He used to wear a swaying moth earring, like a hippie. After he formally became a lawyer, that earring became my possession. I touched my ear; a hand caught my pencil. Francis opened his eyes and smiled at me silently. In the grey-white light, the colour of his eyes became a sombre violet, like the eyes of someone suffocating or dying. For several days we had been exhausted. We had not spoken to the villagers, sleeping soundly in the arranged wooden cabin. Now the dark circles under his eyes had faded considerably, but the fingers touching the back of my hand were still as cold as bone. 依舊，我沒有叫醒弗朗西斯，而是拿起速寫本，翻到新的一頁，開始畫他的臉。世俗上，繪畫是我謀生的手段。而更多時候，繪畫是我對抗告別的方式。我注視弗朗西斯的時間太久，有時會變得不認識這張臉。這時我會閉上眼睛，重新從他的聲音開始建構他。他的眉骨很高，眉尾有一點下垂，讓他看起來總是在思考一件棘手的事情。他的嘴唇只有在笑的時候才會變成另一種形狀。畫到他的下巴時，他翻了個身，把臉埋進枕頭裡，只露出一隻耳朵。我畫了那隻耳朵。耳廓的形狀像一隻收攏翅膀的貝殼，耳垂上有一個小小的凹陷。第一次見他時我就注意過這點。他的耳洞就扎在這個凹陷邊。以前他戴著一隻晃蕩著的飛蛾耳環，像個嬉皮士。在他正式成為律師後，那枚耳墜成為了我的所有物。我摸摸耳朵，一隻手抓住了我的畫筆。弗朗西斯睜開眼，對著我無聲地微笑。灰白色的光線裡，他眼睛的顏色變成一種沉鬱的紫，就像窒息或瀕死的人的眼睛。連續幾天我們都精疲力竭，我們沒有與村人交談，在安排好的木屋中酣眠。此刻他眼下的烏青淡了許多，但他碰著我手背的手指依舊冰冷如骨。
"Good morning." 「早上好。」
He leaned over and kissed my forehead, affectionate like an animal. We walked out of the cabin; the damp grass was silent underfoot. The air was filled with floral fragrance and a faint smell of smoke. This isolated place was full of a soft, nostalgic atmosphere. I closed my eyes and took a deep breath. Francis had already walked a few steps ahead, then turned back to look at me, the corners of his mouth still holding that wicked, sweet curve. His gaze swayed endlessly. 他湊過來親親我的額頭，像動物一樣親暱。我們走出木屋，濕潤的草地踩上去寂靜無聲。空氣中充盈著花香與淡淡的煙味。這個與世隔絕的地方充滿柔軟的懷舊氣息。我閉上眼睛，深吸一口空氣。弗朗西斯在我前方已經走出去幾步，又回過頭看我，嘴角依舊帶有邪惡的甜美弧度。他的眼神晃蕩不止。
"What are you doing? Little dog nose."④ 「你在幹什麼？小狗鼻子。」④
"Smelling. The smell here is special." 「聞。這裡的味道很特別。」
"How is it different?" 「哪裡不一樣？」
"I can't say. Like... like something I smelled when I was little. Also like the smell of your old carpet." 「我說不上來。像……像是小時候聞過的某種東西。也像你家以前地毯的味道。」
Francis did not answer. He walked back to my side, pressed his index finger against my radial artery, and closed his eyes too. He had once said that my pulse could soothe him. When he was tormented by certain cases and could not sleep, he would press his burning forehead against my wrist. I could feel the curve of his cheek gently grinding against my skin. Finally, his restrained lips would peck my wrist. To me, this intimacy was like being preyed upon. 弗朗西斯沒有回答。他走回我身邊，將食指貼在我的橈動脈上，也閉上了眼睛。他曾說過我的脈搏能讓他安心。在他被某些案子折磨得睡不著的時候，他會把滾燙的額頭貼在我的手腕上。我能感受到他側臉的曲線輕柔碾過我的皮膚。最後，他充滿克制的嘴唇會啄我的手腕。對我來說，這種狎暱如同被捕食。
"I can't smell it." 「我聞不到。」
He merely stated, but his voice was so sad. I gripped his hand tightly and walked toward the centre of the village. The person who had greeted us yesterday was an old man with long hair and a long beard. He was now sitting barefoot at the door of a wooden cabin, whittling a piece of wood. He looked like a butcher. The knife was raised like a flag, then swung down without hesitation. As if what he held was a piece of meat. I could not say what kind of tree it was; the wood shavings, fat-white and heavy, fell on his knees. He did not look up, but when our shadows fell on him, he paused, suspending the blade briefly above the wood. 他只是陳述，聲音卻如此悲傷。我握緊了他的手，向村莊中心走去。昨天迎接我們的是一位長髮，長鬍子的老者。此時他正赤著腳坐在一座木屋門口削一塊木頭。他的樣子像一個屠夫。刀像一面旗幟似的舉起來，然後毫不猶豫地一揮而下。彷彿他手裡的是一塊肉。我說不清那是什麼樹，如脂肪般花白厚重的木屑，落在他的膝蓋上。他沒有抬頭，但在我們的影子落在他身上的時候，他停了一下，將刀刃短暫地懸在木頭上方。
"You've come." 「你們來了。」
He spoke in very standard English, his cloudy eyeballs rolling around, pausing only on my face for a long time. He then squinted, as if recognising a face on the street that he had seen a long time ago. 他用很標準的英語說道，渾濁的眼球四處轉動，只在我臉上久久停頓。他旋即瞇起眼，就像一個人在街上認出一張很久以前見過的臉。
"What did you dream?" 「你夢見什麼了？」
As if he did not need an answer, he lowered his head as soon as he asked and continued whittling the wood. Francis's fingers tightened slightly in my palm. 他彷彿不需要一個回答，剛問完就低下頭，繼續削木頭。弗朗西斯的手指在我的手心裡微微收緊了一下。
"What's wrong?" 「怎麼了？」
I tilted my head close to his ear, but he said nothing. We stood silently beside each other, watching until the old man finished whittling the stick, leaned it against the doorframe, and walked back inside. One end of the stick had been carved into the shape of a reindeer antler, its branching outline like a tree growing upside down. I took out my notebook and recorded all of this. This was because sometimes I did not trust my own memory. 我仰頭湊近他的耳朵，他卻一言不發。我們沉默地站在彼此身邊，一直看到那個老人削完了那根木棍，把它靠在門框上，走回屋裡。木棍的一頭被削成了馴鹿角的形狀，分叉的輪廓像一棵倒長的樹。我拿出筆記本，將這一切都記錄下來。這是因為有些時候我並不相信自己的記憶。

At the centre of the circle was another hollow circle, beside which hung a slightly curved cross, its horizontal and vertical arms growing like vines. For some reason, this symbol reminded me of memories I had never possessed. I stood beneath the sun radiating benevolent and primal light, motionless like a pupa. 弗朗西斯拉著我向前走，如生命一樣漫長的白晝中，太陽在我們頭頂微笑。我身邊的木屋如同墳墓一樣寂靜。我停下來，因門柱上刻著的不同尋常的符號駐足。一個圓發出幾道類似光芒的射線。圓心的位置是另一個空心的圓，旁邊掛著一個微微彎曲的十字架，橫臂和豎臂像藤蔓一樣生長著。不知為何，這個符號讓我想起了完全沒擁有過的記憶。我站在散發著仁慈與原始光芒的烈日底下，像一顆蛹似的一動不動。
"Arthur. Arthur." 「亞瑟。亞瑟。」
I came back to myself. Francis stood before me, tilting his head. 我回過神來。弗朗西斯站在我面前，歪著頭看我。
"What's wrong with you?" 「你怎麼了？」
"The light..." 「光……」
I could not continue. Language was flowing out of my throat like a mass of sand. In broad daylight, my mind began to dream. My senses were filled with a furry, warm, scented anomaly, almost suffocating me. At this moment, inspiration was blessing me again, and I greedily bathed in this arrogant grace. My fingers itched. I wanted to draw everything I saw. Everything was metaphor, rhetoric, poetry. And to draw lines according to the metre and rhythm of poetry... no, of animal instinct—that was creation. My sketchbook was in my hand, but I could not lift my hand. The images in my mind swept me away like ocean waves. 我說不下去了，語言正如一團沙子似的從我的喉嚨裡流走。在白天，我的腦子卻開始做夢。我的感官被毛茸茸的、溫熱的、帶有氣味的異狀佔滿，幾乎喘不過氣來。此刻靈感正重新賜福給我，而我也貪婪地沐浴著這種傲慢的恩澤。我的手指在發癢。我想把我看到的一切畫下來。所有的一切都是隱喻，都是修辭，都是詩。而依照詩……不，是動物本能的格律與韻律畫出線條，就是創作。我的速寫本在我的手裡，但我舉不起手，大腦裡的畫面像海浪一樣把我捲走了。

"This place is useful to you." 「這裡對你有用。」
"Mm." 「嗯。」
He was silent for a moment. 他沉默了一會兒。
"But you should go." 「但是你該走了。」

"What animal are you?" 「你是什麼動物？」
I looked at Francis, finding the same bewilderment in his eyes. 我看向弗朗西斯，從他的眼睛裡找出一種同樣的茫然。
"I don't know." 「我不知道。」
The child's eyes brightened. But she suddenly ran off. Francis straightened up and watched the direction the child had run. The man who had helped us return the car yesterday came out of the cabin. He brought us new robes. The material was strange, as soft as silk yet as resilient as linen. When I put it on, it flowed over my body like smoke. Mine was dark blue; Francis's was white. I turned around. Francis was taking off his shirt. His bare back was broad, like some kind of beast. 孩子的眼睛亮了一下。但又突然跑開。弗朗西斯直起身看著那個孩子跑遠的方向。昨天幫我們還車的那個男人從木屋裡出來。他為我們拿來嶄新的長袍。這種料子很奇特，像絲綢一樣柔軟，卻又有亞麻的韌性。當我穿上時，它就像一片煙似的流過我的身體。我的那件是深藍色的，弗朗西斯則是白色的。我回過頭，弗朗西斯正把上衣脫下來。裸露的脊背像某種猛獸般寬大。
"Why should we go?" 「我們為什麼該走了？」
I turned away to tidy up this week. In that instant, Francis had already walked out of the cabin, as if what had just stood there was only an illusion. 我轉過頭去整理這週。而那一剎那，弗朗西斯已經走出屋子，彷彿剛剛站在那兒的只是一陣幻覺。

Dinner was set outside the largest wooden cabin. The cabins here were all round like Eskimo igloos, yet it was impossible to say what style they were. The doors were very low; you had to bend down to enter. The doorframes were carved with those symbols I had seen. The only light source inside came from an iron basin in the centre of the room, where a few pieces of wood burned white. The flames were orange-red like a setting sun, but this place was experiencing the midnight sun, turning the sunset into a distant lie existing only in memory. The red-brown rust on the rim of the iron basin pressed against the colour of the flames; it was impossible to tell where the fire ended and the iron began. The villagers were already seated. No one spoke. Their hands rested on their knees, and they all looked at the fire in unison, saying nothing. The way they gazed was like a form of communication. I was seated beside the old man; the bench made a faint sound like bones rubbing when I sat down. Francis sat opposite me, on the brightest side of the fire. That seat was a little higher than the others, perhaps because he was tall. His shoulders floated out from everyone else's outlines. His face was lit from below by the firelight; the shadows beneath his cheekbones were pushed up above his eyes, turning his eye sockets into two holes. He sat there, back straight, wrists relaxed, fingertips touching fingertips—exactly the posture he used in court. And everyone around him wore animal masks. This scene tightened my throat. We did not wear masks because we had not found our animals. Several girls with the same braids brought in the main course—reindeer meat again, cut into thick slices, drenched in dark red sauce, the juices seeping into the grey-white grain of the meat. I always felt the taste was different from in the city, but I could not say where that aromatic, almost nauseating flavour came from. In the chilling silence, I searched for Francis's eyes. He had barely touched his food again. 晚餐設置在最大的那個木屋門外。這裡的木屋都像愛斯基摩人的冰屋一樣圓，但又說不清是什麼風格。門很低，進去的時候必須彎腰。門框上刻著那些我見過的符號。屋裡唯一的光源來自屋子中央的一隻鐵盆，幾塊木頭燒得發白。火焰橘紅如落日，但這裡正在經歷極晝，使落日變成了一個遙遠的、僅存在記憶裡的謊言。鐵盆的邊緣紅褐色的鏽和火焰的顏色靠在一起，分不清哪裡是火哪裡是鐵。村民已經就座。沒有人說話。他們的手放在膝蓋上，齊刷刷地看著火焰，一言不發。而那種凝視的方式，就彷彿是一種交流。我被安排在那個老人旁邊，條凳坐上去發出細微的、像骨頭摩擦的聲響。弗朗西斯則坐在我對面，火焰最亮的那一側。那個位置比其他人的高出一截，或許是因為他身材高大。他的肩膀從所有人的輪廓裡浮出來。他的臉被火光從下方照亮，顴骨下面的陰影被推到了眼睛上面，眼窩變成兩個洞。他坐在那裡，脊背挺直，手腕放鬆，指尖碰著指尖——和他在法庭上一模一樣的姿勢。而他身邊所有人都戴著動物面具。這副場景讓我的喉嚨發緊。我們沒有戴面具，因為我們沒有找到自己的動物。幾個同樣編辮子的女孩端進來主菜——還是馴鹿肉，被切成厚片，澆著深紅色的醬汁，汁液滲進灰白色的肉紋裡。我總覺得這味道與在城裡時不一樣，但又說不出那種芳香到令人噁心的口感來自何處。我在令人膽寒的沉默中尋找著弗朗西斯的眼睛，他又沒怎麼動食物。

Francis looked at me. His eyes became even more blurred in this question. He smiled at me, and that smile filled my heart with reassurance. I laughed too. Then he let out a great, animal-roar-like laugh. His laughter gave everyone the signal; everyone in the room shrieked with laughter. The laughter made my stomach ache terribly. 弗朗西斯看著我。他的眼睛在這句詢問裡變得更模糊了。他對我笑了笑，那笑容使我心中充滿了安心。我也大笑起來，在一片寂靜中，我的笑聲是生長的荊棘。緊接著，坐在我身邊的老人扭過脖子看我，他的嘴角逐漸向上提，眼睛也瞪大了。然後，他發出一陣巨大的、動物吼叫似的笑聲。他的笑給了所有人信號，屋子裡的所有人都尖聲大笑起來。大笑讓我的肚子劇痛無比。

The sound of breaking branches was like fire burning. 那天晚上，我們走回木屋時聽到了湖的聲音。遙遠的水聲在濕潤的草地上跳躍，彷彿心臟在泥土下的拍打。我跪在草地上，把耳朵貼近地面。湖水應該是拍在了樹根上。枝杈斷裂的聲音，就如同火在燃燒。

During those days, the first thing I did upon waking was not open my eyes, but listen to Francis's breathing. With my eyes closed, I felt his breath falling evenly, like a small bird flying to my forehead. That was the only way I could wake up with peace of mind. Sometimes he was awake, sometimes asleep. When awake, he would look at me. His eyes, forever holding a melancholy expression, hung in their sockets like two grapes pecked hollow by birds. His gaze kissed me; those kisses were clear and divine, yet empty as nothing. 那些天裡，我醒來的第一件事不是睜眼，是聽弗朗西斯的呼吸。我閉著眼睛，感受他的呼吸均勻地下落，像一隻小鳥似的飛到我的額前。那是我能安心醒來的唯一方式。有時候他醒著，有時候睡著。醒著的時候他會看著我，他永遠帶著憂鬱神色的眼睛掛在眼眶中，像兩顆被鳥啄空的葡萄。他的目光親吻著我，那些吻澄澈含神，又空若無物。

"I woke up first." 「我先醒的。」

The memory of fire. Before arriving here, I had seen scenes of dancing around a bonfire on the website. Now, the villagers were beginning to carry birch wood to the fire pit. The bark had been stripped, revealing smooth, pale wood, piled beside the pit like a stack of bones drying in the sun. The women wove wreaths from willow branches, twined with lilies of the valley, wild roses, and lilacs. At certain moments, on the quiet hillside, someone would start without warning, singing a tune without lyrics. It was a seed that had fallen from the sky. No source, everywhere present. The entire village was an animal parasitised by fungus. 火的記憶。到達此處之前，我在網站上看到過篝火旁跳舞的場景。此時，村民正開始往火坑裡搬白樺木。削去了樹皮，露出光滑蒼白的木質，堆在火坑邊上，像一堆正在晾乾的骨頭。女人們用柳條編花環，纏著鈴蘭、野玫瑰和丁香。某個時段，安靜的山坡上會有人毫無徵兆地起頭，開始唱沒有歌詞的調子。幾個如同風聲，鳥獸叫喊的音節，重複著，從木屋之間滲出來，又落入泥土裡，接受烈日的照耀。那是一顆從天上落下的種子。沒有源頭，無處不在。整個村莊是一隻被真菌寄生的動物。
I sat at the door, drawing those birch logs. The paint and paper merged together. I could not draw them. My pen scratched and scratched at the paper, layer after layer of graphite powder piled up, but that was not birch wood. That was not what I saw. I stopped and stared at the pile of wood. Had my body, like layers of overlapping paint, already reached saturation, unable to hold anything more? 我坐在門口，畫那些白樺木。顏料與畫紙融在一起。我畫不出來。我的筆在紙上劃了又劃，鉛粉堆了一層又一層，但那不是白樺木。那不是我看到的东西。我停下筆，盯著那堆木頭。難道我的身體已經如不同疊加的顏料一般趨近飽和，什麼也無法裝下了嗎？

"Can't draw it?" 「畫不出來？」
"Don't laugh at me. Please." 「別笑我。求你。」

"You drew wood. But what you want to draw is fire." 「你畫的是木頭。但你想畫的是火。」

"Francis." 「弗朗西斯。」

"Draw my eyes." 「畫我的眼睛。」
I looked at his eyes. Those two pools of violet sank into bottomless thoughts, like two pieces of glass at the bottom of a lake. I picked up the pen but could not draw the shape. Someone told us not to go near the lake. The lake was not far behind the village, yet no one ever mentioned it. Where the birch forest suddenly parted, the lake and the sky joined into a single cold mirror. The water surface had almost no ripples; the wind only wrinkled the light for a few seconds before escaping from that deathly silence. The lake was huge, so huge that the opposite shore was just a line. Do not go near the lake. But I was walking toward this deep, heavy body of water, like animal bodily fluids. My thoughts were swallowed by the lake, which was why I could draw nothing, only stare at the water surface motionless. The wind carried moisture and the fragrance of distant wildflowers. The more I stared at the lake surface, the more I felt something breathing beneath it. The lake surface exhaled hot, humid vapour; the sound of wind through the leaves was like wailing. I saw countless unsettled souls pressed at the bottom of the lake. These souls were building an underwater Tower of Babel, but this tower had no exit. My fingers began to itch again. Like strata or tree rings, slowly, millimetre by millimetre, crawling upward on my paper. My fingers trembled. Francis said nothing. He just stood beside me, shoulder against shoulder. His body temperature—at this moment, like the lake's temperature—was silent. When I stopped, I was drenched in sweat, soaked all over. "Francis." 「弗朗西斯。」
"Mm." 「嗯。」
"Do you think I belong here?" 「你有沒有覺得我屬於這裡？」

Could not draw it. Could not draw it. I tore out page after page; the paper scraps fell on the rocks and were carried away by the wind. Francis sat beside me, sometimes with his eyes closed, sometimes watching me. His hand occasionally touched the back of my hand, reminding me he was still there. The light changed from grey-white to pale gold, then from pale gold back to grey-white. Dozens of pages had been torn from my sketchbook. Not a single villager approached us; the village was still asleep. But dozens of eyes grew in the forest. I could not describe the feeling of catching living things with my body. Later, I remembered the severed limb that had been stuck in my wheel at the beginning of the journey. Who would murder a deer? The suit, the tie, the briefcase, all the things he had spent half his life putting on, scrambled to leave him. But the head did not. That was his animal instinct, something older, written in the bones—the lion's head does not touch the ground before the body. 獅子倒下的時候，頭顱是最後一個著地的。這很重要。這太重要了。身體先投降。西裝、領帶、公文包、那些他花了半輩子穿上去的東西，爭先恐後地離開他。但頭沒有。那是他作為動物的本能，某種更古老的、寫在骨頭裡的東西——獅子的頭顱不先於身體著地。
I stared—I imagined that image over and over. I was waiting for the lion to close its eyes. But the lion did not. I could not see the closing motion. The lion's savannah—the one that had already begun to recede while Francis was still alive, receding another inch with every defense he made. The gun did not kill the lion. It opened the door to the savannah for the lion. Only the door opened too quickly, too quickly for the lion to tell anyone—the savannah had already dried up. ③ The Sámi are the indigenous people of the Lapland region in northern Finland. Before the arrival of European colonisers, the Sámi had lived on this land known as Sápmi for thousands of years. Their traditional livelihoods centred on reindeer herding, fishing, and hunting; the reindeer thus became the most important symbol in their culture. Sámi culture has a pronounced animistic character. They believe all things have spirits. Natural objects—specific rocks, mountains, lakes, ancient trees—are believed to house deities called "Sieidi," where people make sacrifices for blessings. The sacrifices are usually game animals, or red liquid boiled from alder bark smeared on sacred stones. Red represents the flow of life and also return. Their shamans (Noaidi) use a decorated shamanic drum, the surface of which is painted with the three-layered structure of the cosmos. The shaman enters a trance by drumming, causing the soul to leave the body and travel through different worlds in animal form. The Sámi do not believe the soul necessarily resides in the body. After death, the soul continues to exist in the land of the community in the form of animals, wind sounds, or dreams. The Sámi call themselves "children of the sun." The sun symbol is the most common pattern on shamanic drums and the core of summoning power in healing rituals. Today, the Sámi are one of the oldest surviving indigenous peoples in Europe, with a total population of about 80,000, scattered across Norway, Sweden, Finland, and the Kola Peninsula in Russia. In Finland, the Sámi are a legally protected indigenous people with their own parliament and cultural autonomy. But historically, they have long suffered colonisation, forced Christianisation, language bans, and land dispossession. Today, there are about 9,000 to 10,000 Sámi in Finland. The Sámi and Christianity. From the 17th century onward, the Swedish-Finnish kingdom and the Russian Empire imposed forced Christianisation on the Sámi region. Shamanic drums were burned, sacred stones destroyed, sacred trees felled, shamans arrested, fined, exiled, or even executed. Churches were deliberately built on former sacred sites to cover the traces of the old gods. The Sámi language was excluded from the sacred domain; religious rituals were conducted in Finnish or Swedish. The word of God replaced the word of the forest. But like many forcibly Christianised indigenous peoples, the Sámi retained the skeleton of their old beliefs beneath a surface conversion. Go to church on Sunday, still sacrifice to the sacred stone on Monday. This "dual faith" lasted for centuries, until the 20th century. Today, Sámi Christian belief and their tradition are not necessarily opposed. Many Sámi Christians incorporate shamanic elements into their own churches: shamanic drums replace organs, reindeer antlers are placed on both sides of the altar, sun symbols are embroidered on vestments, juxtaposed with the cross. This is not "heresy," but a way for the oppressed to reclaim their narrative. To tell their own story in the language of the conqueror. But in isolated, more ancient villages, this fusion may move in another direction. There, the two faiths do not coexist peacefully, but slowly, chaotically, unnoticed, devour each other. The sun symbol and the cross grow together; reindeer antlers and the Madonna hang on the same pillar. Fire is no longer merely a symbol of the Holy Spirit—it is the Holy Spirit itself. ⑤ The system of defending murderers. The US Supreme Court established in Gideon v. Wainwright (1963) that for all criminal defendants who may be sentenced to imprisonment, if they cannot afford a lawyer, the state must provide one free of charge. Before that, Powell v. Alabama (1932) had already established the responsibility of the government to appoint defence counsel for poor defendants in capital cases. In that context, if a defendant accused of murder could not afford a lawyer, the court had to appoint one. This means that within the Western legal framework, appointing defence counsel for murderers is not an exception, but a constitutional obligation. Regarding capital cases, take Georgia's Indigent Defence Act as an example. It establishes qualification standards for appointed counsel in capital cases, requiring that the lead defence counsel in a capital case must meet specific experience, training, and competence requirements. In capital cases, the defence counsel appointed by the government is not "randomly assigned," but "meets strict qualification requirements." These lawyers are usually senior criminal defence attorneys with extensive experience in capital defence. In the German legal system, there is a similar institution of necessary defence (Notwendige Verteidigung). Under certain circumstances, a defence counsel must participate in criminal proceedings, including: cases handled at first instance by the Higher Regional Court or the lay judges' court, cases involving charges of felony, cases in pre-trial detention with serious suspicion, etc. In cases of necessary defence, even if the defendant does not apply, the court shall appoint a duty defence counsel ex officio. However, defence counsel in capital cases needs to meet specific qualification standards—usually requiring a certain number of years of criminal defence experience and a certain number of felony or capital case experiences. Similarly, the number of lawyers qualified to defend capital cases is very limited; in some regions, "specialised capital defence lawyers" are a small and closed group. In the public defender system, case assignment is internal. If Francis was systematically assigned to murder cases, perhaps he was trusted to handle the most complex cases—or, as Francis might have realised, he had been "domesticated," becoming part of the system. At the same time, the author believes that the "appointed counsel" system in Western legal systems is noble at the ideal level. But in practice, it produces a kind of schizophrenia. The lawyer's duty is not to "prove the client innocent," but to ensure the fairness of the procedure—to ensure that if the client is to be sentenced to death, it is the conclusion reached after a lawful process. But in the eyes of the public and the victims' families, this lawyer "speaks for murderers." He is a traitor, a moral monster, a person more hateful than the criminal himself. The more professional, serious, and effective he is, the deeper the public's hatred. Francis chose this profession, or this profession chose him. He wears this skin to enter the courtroom, cite precedents, cross-examine. But beneath this skin, the lion has learned to walk in a cage, from one end to the other, and back again. This is a learned stereotyped behaviour. When the victim's family raised the gun, what they saw was not Francis, not a person, not even a lawyer. What they saw was the system itself. 我們是動物。
We Are Animals.
I have always liked watching cult films, but I have also always struggled with how to create a horror atmosphere through words. I am personally very brave; nothing has ever scared me. So I don't know how much of this story will ultimately convey. 我一直喜歡看cult片，但也一直很苦惱怎麼用文字營造恐怖的氛圍。我本人膽子特別大，從來沒有什麼東西嚇到過我。因此我也不知道這個故事最終能表達出多少比例。
This story is inspired by animism, from a reinterpretation of the concept of "soul animals." In many cultural traditions, animal totems are often seen as symbols or guardians—a person resembles a certain animal, or a certain animal protects a person. But in the worldview of this story, it is not that humans possess animal souls, but that animal souls temporarily become human. Personality, profession, desire, trauma—these are merely the folds of that thin human-shaped garment under a particular light. Therefore, the villagers do not ask "Who are you?" but "What animal are you?" The difference between these two is the entrance to the entire world. "Who are you" expects a name, an identity, a history. "What animal are you" expects something else—something about instinct, about dreams. 這個故事受泛靈論啟發，來自對「靈魂動物」概念的一種重新理解。在許多文化傳統中，動物圖騰往往被視為象徵或守護者——人像某種動物，或者某種動物保護著人。但在這個故事的世界觀裡，不是人擁有動物靈魂，而是動物靈魂暫時成為了人。人格、職業、慾望、創傷，都只是那層薄薄的人形外衣在特定光線下的褶皺。因此，村民不會問「你是誰」，而會問「你是什麼動物」。這兩者之間的差別，是整個世界的入口。「你是誰」期待一個名字、一個身份、一段歷史。「你是什麼動物」期待的是另一種關於本能、關於夢境的東西。
Many legends have similar views on animism and external souls, like the Sámi culture borrowed in the text. The soul does not exist entirely within the body. It can be stored in trees, water, fire, or another animal. This means death is not a disappearance, but a displacement. In this story, after a person dies, the soul animal does not immediately disappear. It lingers in the village, appears in dreams, attaches itself between firelight and pollen. Perhaps this explains why Arthur can continue to "see" Francis—not necessarily because of hallucination, nor necessarily because Francis is still alive, but because the soul animal has its own time. But whether the village chose to let him see, or whether he chose to see because he refused to mourn, is left for you to judge. 許多傳說都有泛靈論以及靈魂外置的類似觀點，就像文中借用的薩米文化。靈魂並不完全存在於身體之內。它可以寄存在樹上、水中、火焰裡，或者另一隻動物身上。這意味著死亡不是一種消失，而是一種移位。在這個故事裡，人死後，靈魂動物並不會立刻消失。它會在村莊裡徘徊，出現在夢境中，附著在火光與花粉之間。也許這能解釋為什麼亞瑟能夠繼續「看見」弗朗西斯——不一定因為是幻覺，也不一定因為弗朗西斯還活著，而是因為靈魂動物有自己的時間。但這是村莊選擇讓他看見，還是因為他拒絕哀悼而自我選擇看見，就留給各位評說。
In The Golden Bough, it is described that in rituals, the scapegoat carries away the corruption of the community. But in this story, what the village needs is not a sinner, a villain, a punished person. What they need is a person hollowed out by modern society—tired, empty, sad, severed from the body, severed from dreams, severed from the capacity for empathy. Because this "hollowness" itself can be transformed by fire into new dreams. Burning is not destruction, but translation. Hollowness becomes dream, dream becomes the community's capacity for perception. Arthur's sorrow, exhaustion, loneliness, the crack between him and modern society—these things are his qualifications as a sacrifice. Entering the fire is completing a purifying return. This logic is completely self-consistent within the village, and this self-consistency is exactly where the danger of a cult lies. 《金枝》中描述，在儀式中，替罪羊會帶走共同體的腐壞。但在這個故事裡，村莊需要的不是罪人、惡人、被懲罰的人。他們需要的是被現代社會掏空的人——疲憊、空洞、悲傷、與身體斷裂、與夢境斷裂、與共感能力斷裂的人。因為這種「空洞」本身可以被火轉化為新的夢。燃燒不是銷毀，而是轉譯。空洞變成夢，夢變成共同體的感知能力。亞瑟的悲傷、倦怠、孤獨、與現代社會之間的那道裂縫——這些東西是他作為祭品的資格。進入火中，是完成淨化性的回歸。這種邏輯在村莊內部是完全自洽的，而這種自洽正是邪教最危險的地方。
There is a recurring logic in The Golden Bough: the person who is sanctified must ultimately be killed. Because sacred power decays, abundance moves toward corruption. If it is not renewed, the entire community withers together. In folk horror, sacrifice is often not punishment, but returning the most abundant life to the world. "The king must die." In this story, the "king" is "the person most completely animalised"—the person closest to their soul animal, the person least defined by modern personality, the person most able to share emotions. But not entirely a villager either, because villagers do not have modern diseases. Such a person becomes the centre of the ritual. The essence of the fire sacrifice is to let the soul re-enter the cycle. Arthur is willing to enter the fire because he has once again felt his own soul. 《金枝》裡有一個反覆出現的邏輯：被神聖化的人，最終必須被殺死。因為神聖力量會衰敗，豐盛會走向腐壞。如果不更新，整個共同體會一起枯萎。在folk horror中的獻祭，很多時候並不是懲罰，而是把最豐盛的生命交還給世界。「王必須死」。在這個故事裡，「王」是「動物化最完整的人」——最接近自己靈魂動物的人、最少現代人格的人、最能共享情緒的人。但又不是完全的村人，因為村人不會有現代病。這樣的人會成為儀式的中心。火祭的本質，是讓靈魂重新進入循環。亞瑟願意進入火中，也是因為他重新感受到了自己的靈魂。
Fire is transformation, depersonalisation, the manifestation of the soul, the melting of the boundary between human and animal. Only in the flames of the ritual do the boundaries between human and animal, dead and living, dream and reality temporarily disappear. "Visions in the fire" are not merely hallucinations, but the ritual temporarily altering reality. In the firelight, Arthur can truly see the soul animal, can truly see Francis, can hear that "language without words." Just as the moth does not rush toward the flame for destruction, but because the flame is the only place where it can truly see. 火是轉化，是去人格化，是靈魂顯形，是人獸邊界的融化。只有在祭典的火焰中，人與動物、死者與生者、夢與現實之間的界限才會暫時消失。「火中的幻視」不只是幻覺，而是儀式暫時改變了現實。在火光中，亞瑟可以真正看見靈魂動物，可以真正看見弗朗西斯，可以聽見那種「無語言」的交流。就像飛蛾不是為了毀滅而衝向火焰，而是因為火焰是它唯一能真正看見的地方。
Unlike fire, the prohibition against approaching the lake exists. Unlike the village's cyclical logic, the water surface appears motionless, its cycle invisible. If the village's faith is a cycle, the lake is where the cycle stops. Water enters, but does not leave. No exit, no return. No return, no cycle. No cycle, no life. Approaching the lake is approaching a place where "the cycle has already stopped." For the village, this is an ontological threat—it proves that the cycle is not a necessity of the universe, but only the village's choice. And a choice can be overturned. 與火不同，不要靠近湖水的禁令是存在的。與村莊信仰的循環邏輯不同，水體表面上看是靜止不動的，其循環是不可見的。如果村莊的信仰是循環，湖就是循環停止的地方。水進來了，但沒有離開。沒有出口，就沒有回歸。沒有回歸，就沒有循環。沒有循環，就沒有生命。靠近湖邊，就是靠近一個「循環已經停止」的地方。對於村莊來說，這是一種本體論的威脅——它證明循環不是宇宙的必然，只是村莊的選擇。而選擇是可以被推翻的。
The lake in The Golden Bough also points to personal experience. Frazer repeatedly describes the "tranquility" and "mirror surface" of Lake Nemi—the mirror surface belongs to the viewer alone. The reflection you see on the lake surface is your own, not someone else's. The lapping sound you hear on the shore is heard by your ears, not the community's ears. The lake does not create shared experience; it creates solitary experience. The village's faith aims to eliminate the boundary between individuals. The lake's reflection reinforces the boundary—you can only see yourself in the lake, not others. This is the loneliness in the mirror. 湖在《金枝》中的描述也指向個人經驗。弗雷澤多次描述內米湖的「寧靜」和「鏡面」——鏡面屬於觀看者個人。你在湖面看到的倒影是你自己的，不是別人的。你在岸邊聽見的拍打聲是你的耳朵聽見的，不是共同體的耳朵。湖不創造共享體驗，創造孤獨體驗。而村莊的信仰是為了消除個人與個人之間的邊界。湖面的倒影強化了邊界——你在湖中只能看見自己，無法看見別人。這是鏡中的孤獨。
Regarding sympathetic magic. The Golden Bough describes it as similar things influencing each other. Wearing antlers brings one closer to the deer, imitating animals attracts animal souls, sharing food shares emotions. This story is full of remnants of this logic. Villagers avoid saying true names because names "fix" a person's identity as human. Villagers exchange clothing because scent loosens the boundaries of the soul. Everyone walks barefoot because shoes isolate the dreams in the land. Certain flowers cannot be taken from the village because they will "remember" you. There is no absolute boundary between objects, between humans and animals, between life and death. Everything is fluid. 關於交感巫術。《金枝》描述為相似之物彼此影響。戴上鹿角會更接近鹿，模仿動物會吸引動物靈魂，分享食物會共享情緒。這個故事裡到處都是這種邏輯的殘餘。村民避免說真名，因為名字會把人的身份「固定」為人類。村民交換衣物，因為氣味能讓靈魂邊界鬆動。大家赤腳行走，因為鞋會隔絕土地中的夢。某些花不能帶離村莊，因為它們會「記住」你。物與物之間沒有絕對的邊界，人與動物之間沒有絕對的邊界，生與死之間沒有絕對的邊界。一切都是流動的。
Regarding Francis and Arthur. Francis is a criminal defence lawyer who specialises in defending murderers. The lawyer's work is built on language, rhetoric, and logical manipulation—and the village believes that language is the disease of modern humans. Animals do not need language; they communicate through emotion and perceptual synchrony. Francis's professional exhaustion, his silence, his repression, in the village's view, are his soul animal trying to break free from the constraints of "human personality." Francis is no longer a lion on the savannah, but a beast domesticated by civilisation. Elegant, controlled, suppressing aggression, living long-term in rationality and procedure. As a criminal defence lawyer, he deals daily with violence, lies, trauma, the darkest side of human nature. His "beastliness" has not been released, but trained into a highly civilised exhaustion, like a beast long caged, quiet, polite decline. 關於弗朗西斯與亞瑟。弗朗西斯是一個專門為殺人者辯護的刑辯律師。律師的工作建立在語言、修辭、邏輯操縱之上——而村莊認為，語言是現代人的疾病。動物之間不需要語言，它們通過情緒和感知同步交流。弗朗西斯的職業倦怠、他的沉默、他的壓抑，在村莊的視角裡，是他的靈魂動物正在試圖脫離「人類人格」的束縛。弗朗西斯不再是草原上的獅子，是被文明馴化後的猛獸。優雅，控制，壓抑攻擊性，長期活在理性與程序中。作為刑辯律師，他每天都在處理暴力、謊言、創傷、人性最暗的那一面。他的「獸性」沒有被釋放，而是被訓練成了一種高度文明化的疲憊，就像猛獸長期被關在籠子裡的、安靜的、禮貌的衰竭。
Arthur's soul animal is the moth. Fragile, nocturnal, carrying a kind of powdery uncertainty. Attracted by light, he cannot help but approach. Francis died before the story begins. But the villagers never question the existence of the Francis that Arthur sees; they leave a place for him, speak to the air. Is Arthur hallucinating? Is the village accommodating him? Or does Francis really exist in some form? We cannot determine, but this ambiguity is the core of the story. This cult does not force you to believe something false; instead, it provides a worldview more capable of holding pain than reality. This is also the inspiration from Midsommar. Arthur cannot bear the fact that "he is just dead." The narrative the village provides is: he did not disappear, he simply returned to the animal, to the fire, to the shared dream, to the daylight. This narrative catches Arthur's pain. The village does not even need to deceive him. Arthur completes the rest himself. Arthur finally enters the fire, not because of lover's suicide, but because this is now the only way he can exist. (Regarding the self-deception caused by inability to accept, there is actually a rather terrifying thought: the reindeer meat served at dinner—was it really reindeer, or something else? Only a brain unable to accept reality thinks it is reindeer?) 亞瑟的靈魂動物是飛蛾。脆弱，夜行，帶著一種粉塵般的不確定性。被光吸引，他無法不靠近。弗朗西斯在故事開始前已經死去。但村民從不質疑亞瑟所看見的弗朗西斯的存在，會給他留位置，會對空氣說話。是亞瑟在幻想？是村莊在配合他？還是弗朗西斯真的以某種形式存在？我們無法推斷，但這種模糊是這個故事的核心。這種cult並沒有強迫你相信假的，反而提供了一個比現實更能安放痛苦的世界觀。這也是《仲夏夜驚魂》帶來的啟發。亞瑟無法承受「他只是死了」這個事實。村莊提供的敘事是：他不是消失，他只是回歸了動物，回歸了火，回歸了共同夢境，回歸了白晝。這個敘事接住了亞瑟的痛苦。村莊甚至不需要騙他。亞瑟自己完成了剩下的部分。亞瑟最後進入火中，不是因為殉情，而是因為這現在是他存在的唯一方式。（關於無法接受所導致的自我欺騙，其實還有一個比較恐怖的想法，晚宴上端上來的馴鹿肉，是真的馴鹿，還是其他東西呢？只是無法接受現實的大腦認為那是馴鹿呢？）
The village is in the north, with extremely long daylight, summer never fully entering night. Poor signal, vague maps, rarely appearing in official travel materials. But it will be discovered by "people whose boundaries have loosened"—people in sorrow, people exhausted, people with creative drought, people lonely, people on the verge of mental breakdown. These people will "happen" to see that poster, will "happen" to feel that this road is worth driving into. Only those who no longer quite belong to modern society will be interested in it. And those still firmly rooted in language, profession, identity will not notice its existence at all. 村莊在北方，白晝極長，夏季不完全入夜。信號差，地圖模糊，很少出現在正式的旅遊資料裡。但它會被「邊界鬆動的人」發現——悲傷中的人、倦怠的人、創作枯竭的人、孤獨的人、精神瀕臨斷裂的人。這些人會「恰好」看見那張海報，會「恰好」覺得這條路值得開進去。只有那些已經不太屬於現代社會的人，才會對它產生興趣。而那些還牢牢紮根在語言、職業、身份裡的人，根本不會注意到它的存在。
Finally. This story is not about a cult. Not about murder. Not about the tragedy of "a man tricked into a fire and burned to death." It is about how modern society causes people to gradually lose their soul animals. We are alienated by language, disciplined by profession, fixed by identity, severed from the body and instinct, no longer dreaming, no longer able to perceive others, no longer knowing what animal we are. We are alive, but our soul animals are sleeping, shrinking, disappearing. That village—whether it exists or not, whether it should be called a cult or a community or something else—provides a response: you can become an animal again. You can walk barefoot, you can not speak, you can share dreams, you can cry, you can see the person you have lost in the firelight. The price is that you cannot return as a "modern person." The moth entered the fire. In the firelight, the moth finally saw the lion. The lion also saw him. 最後。這個故事不是關於邪教。不是關於謀殺。不是關於「一個男人被騙進火中燒死」的悲劇。它是關於現代社會如何讓人逐漸失去靈魂動物。我們被語言異化，被職業規訓，被身份固定，與身體和本能斷裂，不再做夢，不再能感知他人，不再知道自己是什麼動物。我們活著，但我們的靈魂動物在沉睡、萎縮、消失。那個村莊——無論它是否存在，無論它應該被稱為邪教還是共同體還是別的什麼——提供了一個回應：你可以重新成為動物。你可以赤腳走路，可以不說話，可以共享夢境，可以哭泣，可以在火光中看見你已經失去的人。代價是，你不能再作為「現代人」回來。飛蛾進入了火中。在火光中，飛蛾終於看見了獅子。獅子也看見了他。
