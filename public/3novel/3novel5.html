Who Killed Dionysus 誰殺死了酒神

◇
At four in the morning, Arthur stood at the door of Francis's apartment. In the hazy fog, the streetlamps blurred like moonlight. It had drizzled for the past few hours; he wiped his soles on the Bohemian-style doormat, took off his gloves, curled his knuckles, and knocked on the wooden door. As a writer, Francis was extremely dependent on late nights and the hours before dawn. In the time when all things fall silent, he could more clearly hear the heartbeat coming from his own chest. So he should not be asleep yet. All was quiet around them, and still no one answered. Arthur did not want to disturb him further, so he fished a spare key from his trench coat pocket and opened the door. Francis's International Klein Blue① wallpaper stung his eyes no matter the light. It reminded him of birds' wings, of heavy sighs like azurite, and also of the universe. His nostrils were filled with a thick smell of coffee; his head was groggy, then brought back down to earth by a separate note of mint. The element of air—promoting energy, communication, and vitality, bringing prosperity to the oasis. He was not surprised that the writer had added it to his night. 凌晨四點，亞瑟站在弗朗西斯的公寓門口。朦朧的霧氣中，路燈暈開如月光。前幾個小時細雨濛濛，他在波西米亞風格的地墊上擦乾鞋底，又將手套脫下，屈起指節叩響木門。作為作家，弗朗西斯極度依賴深夜與凌晨。在萬物都陷入寂靜的時間，他更清楚地聽見自己胸中傳來的心音。因此，他應該還沒睡下。四下裡靜悄悄，此刻仍無人應答。亞瑟不願再打擾他，從風衣口袋裡摸出備用鑰匙開了門。弗朗西斯家的克萊因藍①牆紙無論在何種光線中，都會刺痛他的雙眼。他想起鳥的翅膀、藍銅礦似的沉重嘆息，也想起宇宙。鼻腔被濃重的咖啡味佔據，腦子昏昏沉沉，又被分出的薄荷香氣降回地面。風元素——促進能量、溝通與活力，帶來綠洲的繁榮。他並不奇怪作家把它加入自己的夜晚。
Arthur stepped lightly across the floor, chasing the light spilling from the study. He pressed one finger to his temple. Since yesterday, his temple had been throbbing, like the prelude to a fever. Sometimes he squinted against the pain, flattening his field of vision into a pane of grey glass. In the out-of-focus world, a green moth swept past his eyes and struck the spiderweb in the corner of the ceiling. He looked up and watched the moth struggle until the scales on its body dimmed. Arthur shook his head, took a few steps forward, and pushed the door open. Francis was still sitting at the desk, his back to the door, his head tilted slightly to the left. He seemed to be asleep—just as on countless nights before, he had fallen asleep pillowed on his manuscript. Arthur wanted to go over and drape a blanket over him, but suddenly stopped. Francis's right hand hung below the armrest of the chair, his five fingers relaxed, and beneath his fingertips lay a gun. There was a bullet hole in his left temple; blood ran down his cheek, along his pale, glass-like neck, and pooled on the wooden floor in a dark circular pattern. Arthur pressed his finger to his temple again, walked around to the front of Francis, crouched down, and first touched his hand. The temperature of his skin was colder than the morning; his joints were beginning to stiffen. 亞瑟輕手輕腳地踏過地面，追逐著書房透出的燈光。他將一隻手指按在太陽穴。從昨天開始，他的太陽穴疼痛不止，像是高熱的前兆。有時他瞇著眼睛抵抗這種疼痛，把視野壓成一片灰濛濛的玻璃。失焦的世界中，一隻綠飛蛾掠過他的眼睛，撞上天花板角落的蜘蛛網。他抬起頭觀察飛蛾的掙扎，直到它身上的鱗粉黯淡下去。亞瑟晃了晃腦袋，向前幾步推開門。弗朗西斯依舊坐在書桌前，背對著門，頭微微歪向左側。他像是睡著了——就像以前無數個夜晚一樣，枕著手稿入睡了。亞瑟想過去為他披上一條毯子，卻突然頓住。弗朗西斯的右手垂在椅子扶手下，五指鬆開，指尖下方躺著一把槍。他左太陽穴有一個槍洞，血液順著臉頰，與蒼白的、玻璃似的脖頸流下來，在木地板上聚成深色的圓形圖案。亞瑟又將指頭按向太陽穴，走到弗朗西斯面前，蹲下來，先碰了碰他的手。他皮膚的溫度比清晨還要冷，關節開始僵硬。
He still wanted to drape a blanket over him. 他還是想給他披上一條毯子。
His manner of death was almost enviable to every artist: the last word of his handwritten poem had not yet dried before it was blotted open by blood blooming like a flower. A writer's blood is like ink; a writer's veins flow with poetry. In every writer's most romanticised fantasy, the writer is draped in incomparable golden waves, in sunlight like wine, and dies mingling with words. But they die on the night street. In the winter vegetable market. In a bathtub where the water has gone cold. In their own warm study, with firewood burning. Pillowed on their own last words, asleep. 他的死法幾乎讓每個藝術家嚮往，他手寫詩稿的最後一個字尚未乾透，便被花朵似的綻放的血跡洇開。作家的血就如同墨水，作家的血管裡流淌著詩句。在每個作家最浪漫化的幻想中，作家披著輝煌無比的金波，在如同酒液一般的陽光下，與文字交融著死去。但他們死在夜晚的大街。死在冬天的菜市。死在水已經變冷的浴缸。死在自己溫暖的、燃燒著木柴的書房。枕著自己的遺言酣眠。
The nightingale has finished singing. Sun, please keep shining. 夜鶯已經唱完了，太陽請繼續照耀。
The hand pressing his temple trembled. Arthur pulled the fountain pen from beneath Francis's stiff fingers. He shook it a few times; the ink flew like a happy little bird onto Arthur's expressionless cheek. Blood stained his gloves. He copied Francis's last words into a notebook he carried with him and called the police. He stated the address and his inference, his voice as steady as if he were booking a train ticket. Then he opened another notebook: 按著太陽穴的那隻手顫抖著，亞瑟從弗朗西斯僵硬的手指下抽出鋼筆。他甩了幾下，墨汁像一隻快樂的小鳥，飛向亞瑟沒有表情的臉頰。血液沾上他的手套。他將弗朗西斯的最後遺言抄在隨身攜帶的一本筆記本上，撥了警局的電話。他陳述了地址與自己的推斷，聲音平穩得像在訂一張火車票。旋即打開另一本筆記本：
Francis Bonnefoy, thirty-four years old, poet, theatre critic, occasional culture columnist for a tabloid. Wide social circle but not deep; a few past relationships; living alone for the past year. No record of suicidal tendencies, no medical history, no suicide note. A neighbour said that around eleven last night she heard a muffled thud and assumed a bookshelf had fallen. No one saw a stranger enter or leave. 弗朗西斯·波諾弗瓦，三十四歲，詩人，劇評人，偶爾為小報寫文化專欄。社交圈廣泛但不深入，有過幾段戀情，最近一年獨自生活。沒有自殺傾向記錄，沒有就醫史，沒有遺書。鄰居說昨晚十一點左右聽到一聲悶響，以為是書架倒了下來。沒有人看到陌生人進出。
Arthur's colleagues sealed off the scene, extracted the shell casing, and compared the rifling. Several strangers walked into the room and carried Francis's body away. Some people were flipping through Francis's manuscripts; some pages fell like leaves scattered across the room. 亞瑟的同事將現場封鎖，提取彈殼，比對膛線。幾個陌生人走進了這間屋子，將弗朗西斯的屍體搬走。一些人正在翻看弗朗西斯的手稿，有一些紙片像落葉一樣落在房間各處。
The bullet calibre matched the revolver; only Francis's own fingerprints were on the trigger; the firing distance was determined by the forensic examiner to be pressed tight against the skin, with almost no gunpowder scatter. 子彈口徑與那把左輪匹配，扳機上只有弗朗西斯自己的指紋，射擊距離被法醫鑑定為緊貼皮膚，幾乎沒有火藥散射。
Suicide. 自殺。
Arthur wrote on the report. 亞瑟在報告上寫。
Cause of death pending investigation; the possibility of homicide cannot be ruled out. 死因待查，他殺可能性不能排除。
Arthur wrote in his own notebook. The file was never closed again. 亞瑟寫在自己的筆記本上。卷宗從此沒有被合上過。

That autumn, Arthur returned to the apartment every week. It had almost become his new workplace. The flat was unsealed a month after being sealed off; there was no further new evidence. It was the most ordinary suicide of this era. The landlord asked him to clear out Francis's things, so he packed the books and manuscripts into boxes alone. Nothing else mattered much; in a way, Francis had also been a person of simple living. But the most troublesome part was clearing the enormous bookshelf. Francis had tucked a slip of paper into every poetry collection, marking a certain passage, a certain line, drawing wavy lines in pencil in the margins. Beside some sentences he had drawn a small cross and written "False." Beside others he had drawn a circle and written "True." Whenever Arthur grew tired, he would sit on the mountain of boxes and read the slips one by one. Unfortunately, he could not discern any pattern in these markings. 那一年秋天亞瑟每週都會回到那間公寓，這裡幾乎成了他新的工作地點。房子被封了一個月後便解禁，沒有更多新證據了。這是這個時代最正常的一起自殺事件。房東托他把弗朗西斯的東西清走，他便一個人把書和手稿裝箱。其他東西都不太重要，某種程度上，弗朗西斯也是一個生活簡單的人。但最要命的是清理龐大的書架，弗朗西斯在每一本詩集裡都夾了便簽，標註某一段落，某一行，用鉛筆在頁邊畫波浪線。有些句子旁邊畫了小小的叉，寫著「假。」有些畫了圈，寫「真。」每當亞瑟感到勞累，就坐在堆積如山的箱子上，一張一張讀便簽。很遺憾，他無法看出這些標記有什麼規律。
Death, disappearance, disillusionment, hope, reunion, salvation. 死亡、消逝、幻滅、希望、重逢、拯救。
These words felt foreign to him, like a language he had never heard, already lost. Yet he could not stop turning the pages. The faint musty smell of the pages reminded him of the departed, and so he felt safe. His favourite word was safety. This word had nothing special in pronunciation or spelling—so ordinary, so reassuring. He kept turning until he saw the last page of the last book. Francis had written a passage in red pen; his hand was trembling—the handwriting more scrawled than usual. His hand was trembling. 這些詞語讓他感到陌生，像是一種從未聽過，已經失傳的語言。然而他卻無法停止翻動，書頁翻動時的微黴味使他想起故人，因此感到安全。他最喜歡的詞語就是安全。這個詞在發音和拼寫上都沒有特殊之處，如此普通，如此令人放心。他不停翻動，直到他看見最後一本書的最後一頁。弗朗西斯用紅筆寫了一段話，他的手在發抖——筆跡比平時潦草。他的手在發抖。
Dionysus did not die on the day reason triumphed. Dionysus died on the day he began to believe he should live like Apollo. He sat at his desk, ate on time, wrote no more than three hours a day, went to bed at ten. He no longer danced.② 酒神沒有死在理性勝利的那天。酒神死在他開始相信自己應該像日神一樣活著的那天。他坐在書桌前，按時吃飯，每天寫不超過三小時，晚上十點上床。他不再跳舞了。②
In extreme pain and ecstasy, the individual self dissolves and merges with the source of all things in the universe. The ecstatic no longer knows who he is; he becomes a god, becomes a beast, becomes all otherness. 在極度的痛苦與狂喜中，個體自我瓦解，與宇宙萬物的本源合為一體。迷狂者不再知道自己是谁，他成為了神，成為了獸，成為了所有他者。
Arthur closed the book and did not copy this passage into his notebook. He put the book together with the manuscripts. Six months later the case was formally archived and classified as suicide. 亞瑟合上書，沒有把這段話抄進筆記本。他把書放進和那些手稿放在一起。六個月後案子被正式歸檔，定性為自殺。
"Inspector Kirkland, you can't keep exhausting yourself on a dead man's case. The living need you." 「柯克蘭探長，你不能再為一個死人的案子耗盡心力了，活人需要你。」
"Yes." 「是。」
"You don't look well. Do you want to take leave?" 「你看起來不太好，要不要休假。」
"No need." 「不用。」
Arthur continued to work, getting up at six in the morning, wearing clothes of no more than three colours, writing every report neatly and clearly. He recovered quickly, as if he had never loved that poet. Poets are all madmen; it is a good thing for a normal person to leave a madman. Arthur did not argue. 亞瑟繼續工作，在清晨六點起床，穿顏色不超過三種的衣服，把每一份報告寫得整潔清晰。他恢復得很快，好像他從來就沒愛過那個詩人。詩人都是瘋子，正常人離開瘋子是好事。亞瑟沒有反駁。
In extreme pain and ecstasy, the individual self dissolves and merges with the source of all things in the universe. The ecstatic no longer knows who he is; he becomes a god, becomes a beast, becomes all otherness. 在極度的痛苦與狂喜中，個體自我瓦解，與宇宙萬物的本源合為一體。迷狂者不再知道自己是谁，他成為了神，成為了獸，成為了所有他者。
Arthur did not understand this passage, and he gradually concluded that he had never truly entered Francis's inner world. His work relied on logic, evidence, chains—an action must have a cause, a result must be traceable to a source. He tried to put himself in Francis's position—if a person wanted to die... he would need a reason, a motive, a final spark to push him over. And he found nothing. Francis's death seemed to have no reason. Like an unfinished poem, a line suddenly broken in midair, a sentence grammatically and semantically incomplete. 亞瑟不理解這段話，他逐漸認定自己從未真正走進弗朗西斯的內心。他的工作依賴的是邏輯、證據、鏈條，一個行動必然有前因，一個結果必然能找到源頭。他試著把自己放進弗朗西斯的位置——假如一個人想死……他需要一個理由，一個動機，一個最後推他一把的導火索。而他什麼都沒找到。弗朗西斯的死彷彿沒有理由。就像一首沒寫完的詩，一行突然斷在半空，語法與語義上都未完成的句子。

At least, in memories now marked as meaningless, Francis's behaviour had been frenzied and unpredictable, like the trajectory of particles. During the brief period they lived together, Arthur would stare alone at the light coming through beneath the door. Sometimes the light would abruptly shatter from a wide yellow band into several thin threads—Francis had passed outside the door on his way to get water, without stopping. One rainy three in the morning, the window was not fully closed, and the wind made the curtain billow slightly. In Arthur's dream, a herd of galloping horses swept beneath the moon, their manes shining like violin strings. At the other end of the dream, urgent footsteps came from outside the bedroom door. He snapped his eyes open. Francis stood at the doorway, his shirt sleeves rolled up to the elbows, his hair tied back. 至少，在如今被標為無意義的回忆中，弗朗西斯的行為狂亂而不可預測，如同粒子的運動軌跡。他們短暫同居的那段時間裡，亞瑟一個人盯著門下透過來的燈光。有時光會從一條寬寬的黃帶子，驟然碎裂成幾根細線——弗朗西斯出門倒水時經過了門外，沒有停留。某一個下著雨的凌晨三點，窗戶沒有關嚴，風把窗簾吹得微微鼓起。亞瑟的夢中，一群奔馬在月亮下掠過，鬃毛像琴弦一樣發亮。在夢的另一端，臥室門外傳來急促的腳步聲，他猛地睜開眼睛。弗朗西斯站在門口，把襯衫的袖子挽到手肘。頭髮束在腦後。
"Could you get up for a moment?" 「可以起來一下嗎？」
The light outside the door was mostly blocked by Francis; he looked as heavy as a human-shaped black hole. Arthur's heart plummeted. He looked away, leaned over, and in the darkness grabbed the watch from the bedside table, holding it close to his eyes to see. 門外的光被弗朗西斯擋住大半，他看起來像人形的黑洞一般沉重。亞瑟的心驟然下墜，移開視線探過身子，在一片黑暗中抓起床頭櫃上的手錶，貼在自己眼睛前面試圖看清。
"It is now three seventeen in the morning." 「現在是凌晨三點十七分。」
"I know." 「我知道。」
"You had better know that too—at ten tomorrow morning you have to meet the publisher." 「你最好也知道，明天上午十點你還要去見出版社的人。」
Francis did not answer. He closed the door. In the darkness he walked over, looking even taller, almost like a towering building. He lifted a corner of the blanket and looked at Arthur like a child who did not intend to accept refusal. Arthur sighed, sat up, and reached for the glasses beside the bed. 弗朗西斯沒有回答。他將門關上。黑暗中他走過來，顯得更加高大，幾乎像一座巨廈。他掀開被子的一角，像一個不打算接受拒絕的孩子那樣看著亞瑟。亞瑟嘆了口氣，坐起身，伸手去夠床邊的眼鏡。
He did not take him out. Francis spread a stack of manuscripts on the dining table, turned on the desk lamp, and read Arthur the poem he had just finished. He read quickly, sometimes stopping halfway to grab a pen and change a word, then starting over. The rain outside grew louder; the refrigerator gave a faint hum. Arthur sat opposite, both hands cupping a glass of water that had already gone cold. 他沒有帶著他出門。弗朗西斯把一疊手稿攤在餐桌上，打開檯燈，給亞瑟念自己剛剛寫完的詩。他讀得很快，有時念到一半突然停下來，抓起筆改掉某個詞，又從頭開始。窗外的雨聲逐漸變大，冰箱發出輕微的嗡鳴。亞瑟坐在對面，雙手捧著一杯已經涼透的水。
What the poem was about, he later could not remember. He only remembered a line about birds. Francis read it three times and was dissatisfied each time. He said birds should not fly so lightly; birds have bones, organs, a heavy, constantly beating heart. When they fly, their bodies do not become lighter; they merely temporarily overcome gravity. 那首詩寫了什麼，他後來已經不記得了。只記得其中有一句關於鳥的話。弗朗西斯反覆念了三遍，每一遍都覺得不滿意。他說鳥不應該飛得那麼輕，鳥有骨頭，有內臟，有沉重的、不斷跳動的心臟。它們飛起來的時候身體並不會因此變得輕盈，只是暫時戰勝了重力。
"What is the difference?" 「這有什麼區別。」
Francis looked up, as if hearing for the first time someone ask such a strange question. He looked at Arthur for a long time, and finally laughed. 弗朗西斯抬起頭，像是第一次聽見一個人問出如此奇怪的問題。他看了亞瑟很久，最後笑了起來。
"Of course there is a difference." 「當然有區別。」
He pulled the young inspector's listening heart toward himself. 他將年輕警探那顆聆聽的心拽向自己身邊。
"You cannot assume that a person paid no price simply because he appears to be living well." 「你不能因為一個人看起來活得很好，就認為他沒有付出任何代價。」
Arthur did not answer. He finished the cold water; his stomach ached faintly. He stood up, walked into the kitchen in the darkness, and poured Francis a cup of hot coffee. He also pushed the scattered papers on the table to one side, so that the water stains on the bottom of the cup would not wet them. Francis continued writing poetry, as if that sentence had ceased to matter. Arthur sat on the sofa not far from him, hands on his knees, motionless as a sculpture. When dawn was approaching, they finally returned to the bedroom. Francis turned over in bed and pressed his forehead against Arthur's shoulder. He smelled of coffee, paper, and rain. 亞瑟沒有回答。他把涼水喝完，胃隱隱作痛。他站起來，在一片黑暗中走向廚房，給弗朗西斯倒了一杯熱咖啡。又把桌上散亂的紙整理到一邊，免得杯底的水漬弄濕它們。弗朗西斯繼續寫詩，彷彿剛才那句話已經不再重要。亞瑟坐在他不遠處的沙發上，雙手搭著膝蓋。一動不動，像一尊雕塑。天快亮的時候，他們終於回到臥室。弗朗西斯在床上翻了個身，把額頭抵在亞瑟的肩膀上。他身上有咖啡、紙張和雨水的味道。
"Do you think I am strange?" 「你覺得我很奇怪嗎？」
"Yes." 「是。」
"You should say no." 「你應該說不是。」
"You do not really want to know the answer." 「你不是真的想知道答案。」
Francis laughed once and said no more. He soon fell asleep, his breathing gradually steadying. Arthur kept his eyes open; a thread of grey-white morning light came through the gap in the curtains. There was a report due tomorrow; the coffee cup in the kitchen—Francis certainly had not washed it. Yesterday in his study, he had found that the second screw on the bookshelf had fallen out. When the first one fell, Francis had promised to fix it over the weekend. These things would be done someday. They still had many nights, many early mornings that could be wasted, many times Francis would suddenly get out of bed and demand that he listen to an unfinished poem. He only needed to bend down now and give his tired lover a kiss. What was there to hold onto then? 弗朗西斯笑了一聲，沒有再說話。他很快睡著了，呼吸逐漸變得平穩。亞瑟睜著眼睛，窗簾的縫隙裡透進一線灰白的晨光。有一份報告明天還要交，廚房裡的咖啡杯，弗朗西斯一定沒洗。昨天在他的書房，他發現書架掉了第二顆螺絲釘。第一顆掉的時候，弗朗西斯答應過要在週末修好。這些事情總有一天會完成。他們還有很多個夜晚，很多個可以被浪費的凌晨，很多次弗朗西斯突然從床上爬起來，要求他聽一首還沒有寫完的詩。他只需要在此刻彎下身，給自己疲憊的愛人一個吻。那時能有什麼需要挽留。
◇
Arthur resigned from the police and moved to a small town in the south of France. No one in the town knew him, and he did not try to know anyone. Arthur's rented house was at the end of a slope, with an old olive tree outside the door, its trunk twisted like a tightened rope. In autumn, the olives it bore were small and bitter. Every morning Arthur would sit under the tree and drink a cup of black coffee, watching the mountain ridge line be lit inch by inch by the sunlight. By noon, he would return to the house and continue sorting Francis's manuscripts in a north-facing room, arranging them in chronological order. He had not fully participated in his short life, so he could only judge by patterns. From the early sonnets to the later fragmented emotional passages, the later Francis's sentences grew shorter, the images denser, like a person desperately stuffing too much into a bottle until the bottle breaks. 亞瑟從警局辭職，搬到了南法一個小鎮。鎮上沒有人認識他，他也沒去認識任何人。亞瑟租的房子在一條斜坡的盡頭，門外有一棵老橄欖樹，樹幹扭曲如絞緊的繩索。秋天，樹上結的橄欖小而澀。亞瑟每天早晨會坐在樹下喝一杯黑咖啡，看遠處的山脊線被陽光一寸一寸照亮。到中午，他就回到屋子，繼續在一間朝北的房間裡整理弗朗西斯的手稿，按照時間順序排列。他沒有完整地參與他短暫的人生，只能憑規律判斷。從早期的十四行詩到後期散碎的情緒片段，越到後期，弗朗西斯的句子越短，意象越密集，像一個人拼命往一個瓶子里塞太多東西，直到瓶子破裂。
It took him two years to sort through all of Francis's posthumous works. The poems were numbered by year, the essays and reviews ordered by publication, the unfinished fragments bound separately. He even wrote annotations for each poem, noting allusions, metaphors, possible writing contexts. Until this gradually ceased to be a poetry collection and became a dossier of evidence. Yes, it was. It was a forensic examiner dissecting a butterfly. 他花了兩年時間整理弗朗西斯的全部遺作。詩稿按年份編號，散文和評論按刊物排序，未完成的片段單獨成冊。他甚至還給每一首詩寫了註釋，標註出典、隱喻、可能的寫作背景。直到這漸漸不再是一本詩集，而是一本證據卷宗。是，它就是。是一個法醫在解剖一隻蝴蝶。
Essay One: "The Fear of Noon." 隨筆一：《正午的恐懼》。
I am afraid of the sun rising to its highest point, but the fear has nothing to do with UV damage. I fear that feeling of being completely exposed, like being dissected. Walking down the street, I feel that everyone can see through my skull, can see those shapeless, buzzing things in my brain. 我害怕太陽升到最高處，但恐懼與紫外線的傷害無關。我怕那種被一覽無遺的感覺，如同被解剖的感覺。我走在街上，覺得每個人都能看穿我的顱骨，看見我腦子里那些不成形狀的、嗡嗡作響的東西。
When I was young, I thought that was inspiration. I thought that irresistible urge to scream or dance or flip the table was talent. I was proud of it. I stayed in taverns until dawn, drank every drop of alcohol I could find, kissed lovers, recited poetry by the river against the wind. Back then I thought I was a child of words, that night was my mother, that chaos was my milk. 年輕時我以為那是靈感。我以為那種難以抑制的、想要尖叫或者跳舞或者把桌子掀翻的衝動是天賦。我為此驕傲。我在酒館裡待到凌晨，喝光所有能找到的酒，和情人接吻，在河邊迎著風朗誦詩歌。那時候我覺得自己是文字的孩子，黑夜是我的母親，混亂是我的乳汁。
Now I know. That was not talent. It was an undiagnosable, intermittent illness that made a person sit up suddenly at three in the morning feeling that he must immediately write ten thousand lines of poetry or suffocate. The doctor says I need a regular routine. Friends say I need to find a goal. Arthur says I need... 現在我知道了。那不是什麼天賦。那是一種無法被診斷的、間歇發作的、讓人們在凌晨三點突然坐起來覺得自己必須立刻寫下一萬行詩，否則就會窒息的病。醫生說我需要規律作息。朋友們說我需要找到一個目標。亞瑟說我需要……
Arthur says nothing. Arthur gets up at six every day, makes the bed neatly, puts the cups washed last night back in their place, and goes out. He comes back at ten at night, washes the cups again, and puts them in the same place. Arthur is like a clock. I have never seen him lose control, never seen him drunk, never seen him sit up suddenly in the middle of the night and say "I thought of a line, I have to tell you." His sleeping posture is like a supine sculpture, hands folded on his abdomen. 亞瑟什麼也沒說。亞瑟每天六點起床，把床鋪整齊，把昨晚洗好的杯子放回原位，然後出門。晚上十點回來，再洗一遍杯子，放在同一個位置。亞瑟像一座鐘。我沒有見過他失控，沒有見過他醉酒，沒有見過他深夜忽然坐起來說「我想到了一句話，必須告訴你」。他睡覺的姿勢像一尊平躺的雕塑，雙手交疊放在腹部。
I fucking love him like this. 我他媽愛他這樣。
I hope I can move closer to him. Eat on time, write on time, not call anyone at three in the morning. But for three months, I have not written a single sentence I am satisfied with. Those things are still there, but they no longer speak; they just scream in my head over and over, repeatedly, meaninglessly. 我希望自己能向他靠近。按時吃飯，按時寫作，不在凌晨打電話給任何人。可是三個月，我沒有寫過一句讓我自已滿意的句子。那些東西還在，但它們不再說話了，它們只是在我腦子里反覆地、重複地、毫無意義地尖叫。
Today at noon I passed the Church of Saint-Sulpice.③ The sunlight made the square glaringly white, and I had no shadow. I suddenly thought: if I killed myself at noon, the corpse would have no shadow. The sun would absorb me entirely, like absorbing a drop of water. 今天中午我經過聖敘爾比斯教堂③，陽光把廣場照得白晃晃的，我沒有影子。我忽然想到，如果我在正午把自己殺死，屍體不會有影子。太陽會把我整個人吸走，像吸走一滴水。
In the last period they spent together, Francis smiled more, discussed the weather with ease, made coffee as well as a barista. He would kiss the corner of Arthur's mouth when they parted. Once, at dinner, Francis told friends that he had been feeling much more stable lately, finally like an adult. Friends raised their glasses in congratulation, the glasses filled with wine. Francis raised his teacup and clinked with them. 在他們最後相處的時光中，弗朗西斯笑容變多，從容地討論天氣，咖啡煮得像咖啡師一樣好。他會在分別時吻他的嘴角。甚至在一次晚餐上，弗朗西斯對朋友說他最近感覺穩定多了，終於像個成年人了。朋友們舉杯祝賀，杯裡斟滿酒。弗朗西斯舉起茶杯與他們捧杯。
"No, I love him like that." 「不，我愛他那樣。」
Arthur retorted at the time. 那時亞瑟反駁道。
One afternoon he did not continue sorting but walked out the door and down the slope. He passed vineyards ripening into ecstasy; rosemary bloomed in the shadows of the vines. He walked all the way to the cemetery at the edge of the town. More than twenty rows of gravestones were arranged along the gentle slope, the limestone warmed by the Mediterranean sun into a warm ivory white. He sat down on a bench, his back against an angel sculpture with spread wings. It made him feel safe here. In the peace, he closed his eyes and remembered the first thing he had done at the scene that day—crouching down and gently holding Francis's hanging hand. The temperature at the fingertips was still there, like freshly extinguished furnace ash. At that moment he knew it was not homicide. No murderer would, after pulling the trigger, place the gun so gently beneath the victim's fingers, as if returning an instrument. But he still wrote "the possibility of homicide cannot be ruled out." He knew what he was doing; he set up procedural obstacle after procedural obstacle to accepting it. As long as the case was not classified, Francis remained in a pending state, not completely disappeared. 某天下午他沒有繼續整理，而是走出門，沿著山坡向下。他經過葡萄成熟、陷入狂喜的葡萄園。藤條的陰影底下迷迭香綻放。他一直走到小鎮邊緣的墓園。二十幾排墓碑沿著緩坡排列，石灰岩被地中海陽光曬成溫暖的米白色。他在一張長椅上坐下，背靠一座張開翅膀的天使雕塑。這裡讓他覺得安全。他在一片祥和中閉上眼，回忆起案發當天自己在現場做的第一件事——蹲下來，把弗朗西斯垂下的那隻手輕輕握了一下。指尖的溫度還在，像剛熄滅的爐灰。他在那一刻就知道這不是他殺。沒有任何一個凶手會在扣動扳機後，再以如此溫柔的姿態把槍輕放在受害者的手指下面，就如放回一件樂器那般。但他依然寫了「他殺可能性不能排除」。他知道自己在做什麼，他給接受這件事設置了一道又一道程序性障礙。只要案子還沒定性，弗朗西斯就還處在待查狀態，就不算徹底消失。
He heard the wind rising and got up to walk back to his lodgings. Dusk dyed the entire olive grove the colour of rust; the wind carried the smell of burning wood. The moment he pushed open the door, he saw the manuscript spread on the desk stirred by the wind, the pages rustling, like a bird trapped in the room. 他聽見風聲漸起，便起身走回住處。黃昏把整片橄欖樹林染成鐵鏽色，風裡有燒柴的味道。他推開門的瞬間，看見書桌上攤開的手稿被風吹動，紙頁嘩啦啦地翻著，像一隻被困在房間裡的鳥。
Essay Two: "The Last Page." 隨筆二：《最後一頁》。
I have decided to admit my former mediocrity. 我決定承認自己從前的平庸。
It took me a long time to understand that the first thing an adult should learn is to manage himself, not to express himself. Expression is the privilege of the young; management is maturity. 我花了很多時間才明白，一個成年人首先應該學會的是管理自己，而不是表達自己。表達是年輕人的特權，管理是成熟。
For a while now, I have lived according to a plan. Fixed time to get up, fixed time to sleep, no alcohol, no late nights. I have learned to schedule work during the day and handle emotions when alone. I have begun to believe that routine is more reliable than inspiration. 過去一段時間，我按照計劃生活。固定時間起床，固定時間睡覺，不飲酒，不熬夜。我學會了把工作安排在白天完成，把情緒留在獨處的時候處理。我開始相信，規律比靈感更可靠。
It has proven to be genuinely good. 事實證明，這樣確實很好。
My life has finally stabilised. I no longer leave the dining table because of a sudden sentence, nor cancel the next day's plans because of a dream. I have learned to maintain appropriate silence in social situations, to listen carefully when others speak, and when it is my turn, to say things that will not embarrass anyone. I have become gentler and easier to get along with. They were right. 我的生活終於穩定下來。我不會再因為一句突然出現的話離開餐桌，也不會因為一個夢取消第二天的安排。我學會了在社交場合保持恰當的沉默，別人說話的時候認真傾聽，輪到我時說一些不會讓任何人為難的話。我變得溫和了，也更容易相處了。他們是對的。
In recent months, I have not written a single poem I am satisfied with. 最近幾個月，我沒有寫出任何一首滿意的詩。
At first I thought it was temporary. Later I found that it was not really that important. The world does not need me to write another poem; the publisher stays open as usual, the newspaper is published as usual, the coffee goes cold as usual, the sun rises every day. I have finally learned not to take myself too seriously. 起初我以為只是暫時的。後來我發現，其實也沒有那麼重要。世界並不需要我再寫一首詩，出版社照常營業，報紙照常發行，咖啡照常變涼，太陽每天都會升起。我終於學會了不要把自己看得太重要。
Today at noon I passed the church again and sat in the square for a while. The sunlight was good, people came and went, everything was orderly. I suddenly felt that this was also very good. There was nothing I needed to chase, and no voice urging me to go home immediately. 今天中午我再次經過教堂，在廣場上坐了一會兒。陽光很好，人們來來往往，一切井然有序。我忽然覺得這樣也很好。沒有任何東西需要我去追趕，也沒有任何聲音催促我立刻回家。
My mind has finally grown quiet. 我的腦子終於安靜了。
I should be happy about this. 我應該為此感到高興。
A person who can work on time, rest on time, eat on time, cause no trouble to anyone, create no chaos, expect no miracles, believe in no talent, and no longer mistake those groundless impulses for oracles. 一個人能夠按時工作，按時休息，按時吃飯，不給任何人添麻煩，不製造混亂，不期待奇蹟，不相信天賦，也不再把那些毫無根據的衝動誤認為神諭。
I have finally become a normal person. 我終於成為了一個正常的人。
A normal person is an ideal, complete person. 一個正常的人，就是一個理想中的完人。
That night Arthur slept lightly. He dreamed that Francis stood in the noon square, the sunlight stabbing straight down like dozens of knives piercing his body. Francis had no shadow. Arthur walked over and called him in a hoarse voice. Francis turned his head, his face full of sorrowful bewilderment, his lips slightly parted. Arthur leaned in to listen but heard only a sustained high-frequency hum, like overloaded wires or a giant flying insect struggling inside a lightbulb. He woke in that hum, cold sweat sticking the sheets to his back. 那一夜亞瑟睡得很淺。他夢見弗朗西斯站在正午的廣場上，陽光垂直地刺下來，像數十把刀穿透他的身體。弗朗西斯沒有影子。亞瑟走過去，用沙啞的聲音喚他。弗朗西斯轉過頭來，臉上充滿悲傷的迷惘，嘴唇微微張開。亞瑟湊近去聽，卻只聽見一陣持續的高頻嗡鳴，像電線過載或是一隻巨大的飛蟲困在燈泡裡掙扎。他在那陣嗡鳴中醒來，冷汗讓床單黏在了背上。
He was the maker of the gun. 他是手槍的製造者。
He. 他。
He stopped, realising he was using the third person, as if describing a person unrelated to himself. He crossed out "he" and wrote "I" beside it. Then crossed out "I" and wrote "Arthur." Then crossed it out again, leaving an ink-stained hole like a through-and-through gunshot wound. Dawn light leaped into the room; the outline of the olive tree slowly developed from the darkness. Arthur suddenly felt an absurd calm. He went downstairs, walked barefoot into the kitchen. He cut tomatoes into chunks, tore basil, tossed with olive oil and sea salt, sliced bread thick and toasted it in the oven until the edges were crisp and charred. He sat under the olive tree and finished the meal in the newborn sunlight. The olive tree's shadow fell on the table, slowly rotating with the sun's movement, like a silent sundial. 他停住了，意識到自己在使用第三人稱，像在描述一個和自己無關的人。他把「他」劃掉，在旁邊寫了「我」。然後又把「我」劃掉，寫了「亞瑟」。然後又劃掉，留下一個墨跡斑斑的、貫穿槍傷似的空洞。黎明的光線跳入屋子，橄欖樹的輪廓從黑暗中慢慢顯影。亞瑟忽然感到一陣荒謬的平靜。他下了樓，赤腳走進廚房。他把番茄切塊，羅勒撕碎，拌上橄欖油和海鹽，麵包切成厚片在烤箱裡烤到邊緣焦脆。他坐在橄欖樹下，就著初生的陽光吃完了那頓飯。橄欖樹的影子投在桌面上，隨著太陽的移動緩緩旋轉，像一個沉默的日晷。
After eating, he did not wash the dishes; he simply left the plate on the table. He went back upstairs to the bedroom, opened the desk drawer. Inside lay a revolver—after the case he had retrieved it from the evidence room; by regulation it should have been permanently sealed, but he had not done so. He had stolen it. 他吃完後沒有洗碗，就那麼把盤子留在桌上。他重新上樓走到臥室，拉開書桌抽屜，裡面放著一把左輪手槍——案發後他從證物室調出來的，按規定應該被永久封存，但他沒有。他把它偷走了。
He laid the gun on the table. The first step of investigation is to discover the truth; the second step is to admit the truth; the third step is—he did not know what the third step was. Perhaps the third step was just to sit here, a gun in hand, waiting for something that had not yet happened. Sunlight came through the window, cutting the tabletop into light and dark halves; half the gun's body reflected light, half sank into shadow. In the distance came the church bells; pigeons paced on the roof, cooing. The neighbour next door was playing the radio; a female voice sang lazily about summer. Life was as usual. He brought the muzzle close to his temple. 他把槍扣在桌上。探案第一步是發現真相，第二步是承認真想，第三步是——他不知道第三步是什麼。也許第三步就是坐在這裡，手裡握著一把槍，等待某一件還沒有發生的事情發生。陽光從窗外照進來，把桌面切成明暗兩半，槍身一半反光，一半沉在陰影裡。遠處傳來教堂的鐘聲，鴿子在屋頂上踱步，咕咕地叫著。隔壁鄰居在放收音機，一個女聲慵懶地唱著關於夏天的句子。生活一切如常。他把槍口湊近自己的太陽穴。
"Bang." 「砰。」
He did not pull the trigger. He moved his hand away from the gun, closed the notebook, took off his trench coat and hung it back in the wardrobe. He went downstairs, washed the dishes, dried them, put them back in place. Then he put on his shoes, locked the door, and walked toward the centre of the town. He needed to buy a bag of new coffee beans. The coffee beans were almost gone. In the distance children were laughing, a small dog was jumping about on the lawn, a motorcycle rumbled up the slope from below and drove down again. All these sounds intertwined, merging into a soft, dense, utterly unprofound noise. Arthur walked in the middle of the noise, calm and quiet. 他沒有扣動扳機。把手從槍上移開，合上筆記本，把風衣脫下來掛回衣櫃。他下樓，洗了碗，擦乾，放回原位。然後他換上鞋，鎖上門往小鎮的中心走去。他要買一包新的咖啡豆。咖啡豆快用完了。遠處有孩子在笑，小狗在草坪上跳來跳去，一輛摩托車從山坡下轟鳴著駛上來，又駛下去。所有這些聲音交織在一起，匯成一種柔軟的、綿密的、毫不深刻的噪音。亞瑟走在噪音中間，從容而安靜。
He stopped at the door of the coffee shop and pushed it open. The bell on the door rang once; the coffee aroma inside was rich and safe. 他在咖啡店門口停下腳步，推開門。門上的鈴鐺響了一聲，店裡的咖啡香氣濃郁而安全。
"Black coffee. One cup." 「黑咖啡。一杯。」
Then he paused, as if suddenly remembering something, and added: 然後他頓了頓，像是忽然想起什麼，又補了一句：
"And a caramel coffee." 「加一杯焦糖咖啡。」
He carried two cups of coffee out of the shop. The sun was slanting west; the olive tree's shadow grew longer and longer, eventually joining with his own. He turned and walked back. The town's lights came on one by one, warm yellow light spilling from the windows, dyeing the stone path the colour of honey. Arthur carried a bag of newly bought coffee beans, the hem of his trench coat swaying gently in the evening wind. He walked into the house, put the coffee beans into the jar, and placed the jar where it should be. He sat down at the desk, opened the file, and drew a horizontal line at the very bottom. 他端著兩杯咖啡走出店門。陽光西斜，橄欖樹的影子越來越長，最終與他的影子連在一起。他轉身往回走。小鎮的燈一盞一盞亮起來，暖黃色的光從窗戶裡透出，把石板路染成蜂蜜色。亞瑟手裡拿著一包新買的咖啡豆，風衣下擺在晚風中輕輕擺動。他走進屋，把咖啡豆放進罐子里，把罐子放在它應該放的位置。他坐到書桌前，翻開卷宗，在最下方畫了一條橫線。
Case closed. 結案。
He closed the notebook, turned off the light, and sat in the darkness for a while. The moon had risen outside the window; moonlight fell on the olive tree, and the tree's shadow was reflected on the window glass, like a blurred human shape. The wind paused for a moment; the olive branches stood still like a sculpture, and moonlight fixed everything in that instant—the cup, the table, the chair, the closed notebook. Then the wind suddenly rose again, the leaves trembled anew, the moon moved past the window frame, the shadow lengthened and shortened. He picked up Francis's manuscript again. 他合上筆記本，熄燈，在黑暗中坐了一會兒。窗外的月亮升起來了，月光照在橄欖樹上，樹影映在窗玻璃上，像一個模糊的人形。風停了一瞬，橄欖樹的枝葉靜止如雕塑，月光把一切都固定在那個瞬間裡——杯子，桌子，椅子，合上的筆記本。然後風突然又起了，樹葉重新顫動，月亮移過窗格，影子變長又拉短。他又拿起弗朗西斯的手稿。
I tried to become you.
But there were too many voices in my body, and at noon they all died. 我試圖成為你。
但我的身體裡有太多聲音，它們在正午時分全部死了。
Arthur put down the manuscript and went to the kitchen to pour himself a glass of water. He saw himself in the mirror—gaunt face, sharply defined cheekbones, deeply sunken eye sockets. He was looking more and more like another person. He remembered that when Francis was alive he had once said: Arthur, your face is like a perfect sculpture, but unfortunately sculptures cannot say "I love you." Arthur replied, I have said it. Francis laughed and said, you have said it, but when you said it, it sounded like you were reading a statement. That night Arthur did not sleep. He sat at the desk, before him his own investigation notes, a thick volume, every page filled with dates, locations, physical evidence, witness testimony, reasoning. He turned to the last page, where he had written the case closure that evening. 亞瑟放下手稿，走到廚房給自己倒了杯水。他看見鏡子裡的自己，面孔瘦削，顴骨輪廓清晰，眼窩深陷。他越來越像另一個人。他想起弗朗西斯活著的時候曾經說過，亞瑟，你的臉像一座完美的雕像，可惜雕像不會說「我愛你」。亞瑟回答，我說過。弗朗西斯笑了，說，你是說過，但你說的時候像在宣讀一份證詞。那天晚上亞瑟沒有睡。他坐在桌前，面前攤開的是他自己做的調查筆記，厚厚一冊，每一頁都寫著日期、地點、物證、人證、推理過程。他翻到最後一頁，那裡還有他傍晚寫上的結案。
He picked up the pen and wrote a line beneath the case closure. His hand did not tremble; the handwriting was as neat and clear as ever: 他拿起筆，在結案下面寫了一行字。他的手沒有發抖，字跡一如既往地端正、清晰：
"This is my statement. I killed him." 「這就是我的陳述。我殺了他。」
Arthur turned back to look at you. He said nothing. 亞瑟回頭看向你，他什麼也沒說。
① International Klein Blue (IKB) is the signature blue developed by French artist Yves Klein. In the late 1950s, he worked with technicians to explore a formula that could preserve as much as possible the intensity and texture of ultramarine pigment, establishing it as the representative colour of his artistic practice under the name International Klein Blue. This blue is closely associated with his exploration of pure colour, the void, and infinity. ① 克萊因藍（International Klein Blue，IKB）是法國藝術家伊夫·克萊因發展的標誌性藍色。1950年代後期，他與相關技術人員合作，探索一種能夠盡可能保留群青顏料濃烈色彩與質感的配方，並以國際克萊因藍之名確立其藝術實踐中的代表性色彩。這種藍色與他對純粹色彩、虛空及無限性的探索密切相關。
② The Apollonian and the Dionysian are two artistic forces proposed by German philosopher Friedrich Nietzsche in The Birth of Tragedy. The Apollonian is associated with form, boundaries, individual images, and dreamlike contemplation; the Dionysian is associated with intoxication, music, the dissolution of individual boundaries, and fusion with the source of life. Nietzsche believed that the artistic power of Greek tragedy arose from the conflict and union of the two, rather than the simple replacement of one by the other. ② 日神與酒神（The Apollonian and the Dionysian）是德國哲學家弗里德里希·尼采在《悲劇的誕生》中提出的兩種藝術力量。日神與形式、界限、個體形象及夢境般的觀照相關；酒神則與醉狂、音樂、個體界限的消融，以及與生命本源的融合相關。尼采認為，希臘悲劇的藝術力量產生於二者的衝突與結合，而非其中一方對另一方的簡單取代。
③ The Church of Saint-Sulpice is located in the Saint-Germain-des-Prés area of the 6th arrondissement of Paris and is one of the important historic churches of Paris. The existing building was begun in the mid-17th century and underwent construction and expansion over several centuries, blending Gothic architectural tradition with classical style. The interior is known for its vast space, rich artistic decoration, and murals by Delacroix. ③ 聖敘爾比斯教堂（Église Saint-Sulpice）位於法國巴黎第六區聖日耳曼德佩一帶，是巴黎重要的歷史教堂之一。現存建築始建於17世紀中葉，歷經多個世紀的修建與擴建，融合了哥特式建築傳統與古典主義風格。教堂內部以宏大的空間、豐富的藝術裝飾及德拉克洛瓦創作的壁畫而聞名。
At first I just wanted to write some of my own feelings. I do not like being misunderstood. Or, more precisely, I do not like my articles being hastily summarised and lightly passed over before they have been truly understood. A writer puts his emotions into words, thinking that at least in this way certain things that cannot be said directly can be given a shape. But expression does not mean arrival. You can spend a very long time depicting a thing, and another person only needs a very short time to walk past it. This inability of the soul to reach another was placed between the two people, and so "Who Killed Dionysus" came to be. 最初我只是想寫一些自己的感受。我不喜歡被誤解。或者更準確地說，我不喜歡我的文章尚未被真正理解，便已經被匆匆概括、輕輕帶過。寫作者將自己的情感放進文字裡，以為至少可以藉此讓某些無法直接說出口的東西獲得形狀。可是表達並不意味著抵達。你可以花費很長時間去描繪一個事物，而另一個人只需要很短的時間，就能從它身邊走過去。這種心靈上的無法抵達被放在了兩個人中間，於是才有了《誰殺死了酒神》。
I do not think the problem between Arthur and Francis is how deeply they loved. They did love each other, and they did try to understand each other. That two such different people were willing to draw close is a kind of courage, and also a kind of foolishness. We are good at mistaking the act of drawing close for understanding, mistaking living together for sharing the same world. We forget that even when two people already share many memories, they may still feel entirely different things about the same event. 我不認為亞瑟與弗朗西斯之間存在愛得深淺的問題。他們確實相愛，也確實嘗試過理解彼此。兩個如此不同的人願意相互靠近，是一種勇氣，也是一種愚蠢。我們善於將靠近的動作誤認為理解，將共同生活誤認為共享同一個世界。我們忘記了即使兩個人已經擁有許多共同的記憶，他們仍然可能對同一件事懷有截然不同的感受。
Love cannot guarantee understanding. It cannot even guarantee that a person will preserve himself within love. 愛無法保證理解。它甚至不能保證一個人會在愛裡保全自己。
I regret that what I wrote is a change that no longer insists on the self, an ending of devouring. Here no one intended to hurt anyone from the beginning, and there is no easily identifiable sinner. There is only constant compromise, adjustment, trying to let life continue, until one day, one discovers that those things once irreplaceable have quietly changed. And when loss finally happens, those who remain may not know what exactly they have lost, or what they once participated in. So I borrowed the imagery of the Apollonian and the Dionysian. They provide a classical shape for this relationship, but they do not solve its problems for me. I do not want to fix the two people respectively as embodiments of certain ideas, nor to explain a death with a philosophical proposition. Rather than seeking a definite answer, I care more about this: when two people have already tried their best to draw close yet still cannot understand each other, what remains of their love? And when one person ends everything in an extreme way, can the other finally understand him? I think not necessarily. 我很遺憾我寫的是一種不再堅持自我的改變，一種吞噬的結局。這裡沒有誰從一開始就打算傷害誰，也沒有一個能夠輕易指認的罪人。有的只是不斷地妥協、調整、試圖讓生活繼續，直到某一天，才發現那些曾經不可替代的東西已經悄然改變。而當失去終於發生，留下來的人也未必知道自己究竟失去了什麼，又曾經參與了什麼。所以我借用了日神與酒神的意象。它們為這段關係提供了一種古典的形狀，卻沒有替我解決其中的問題。我並不想把兩個人分別固定為某種觀念的化身，也不想用一個哲學命題解釋一場死亡。比起尋找一個確定的答案，我更在意的是當兩個人已經盡力靠近，卻依然無法理解彼此時，他們的愛還剩下什麼？而當一個人以極端的方式結束一切，另一個人是否就終於能夠明白他？我想未必。
Arthur tries to reassemble Francis through memory, evidence, and statement. He needs a cause of death, and he needs someone who can bear the guilt. In the end, he assigns the guilt to himself. But admitting that he is the murderer does not mean he has truly understood the dead. Sometimes people would rather believe that tragedy must have an identifiable cause than accept that some distances were never crossed. 亞瑟試圖通過記憶、證據與陳述重新拼湊弗朗西斯。他需要一個死因，也需要一個可以承擔罪責的人。最終，他把罪名歸給了自己。但承認自己是凶手也並不意味著真正理解了死者。有時人寧願相信悲劇必定有一個可以指認的原因，也不願接受有些距離從來沒有被跨越。
And Francis, too, never fully understood Arthur. 而弗朗西斯又何嘗不是從未完全理解亞瑟。
As for myself, when I first wrote this story there was no such grand reason. I just wanted to express some things that were important to me. I hope they can be taken seriously, rather than being simplified into an easy conclusion before they have even unfolded. I do not demand that everyone like what I write, nor do I believe that expression itself can earn understanding. But I still care about what actually happens when those words reach another person. Perhaps writing is just this kind of contradictory attempt. Knowing that you cannot control how others understand you, yet still willing to hand certain things over. Knowing that language cannot eliminate the distance between people, yet still trying to give that distance a shape. 而我自己，寫下這個故事的最初並沒有這麼宏大的理由。我只是想表達一些對我而言重要的東西。我希望它們能夠被認真對待，而不是在尚未展開之前就被簡化成一個輕易的結論。我並不要求每個人都喜歡我寫的東西，也不認為表達本身就能換來理解。但我仍然在意那些文字抵達另一個人時究竟發生了什麼。也許寫作就是這樣一種矛盾的嘗試。明知道無法控制別人如何理解自己，卻仍然願意把某些東西交出去。明知道語言無法消除人與人之間的距離，卻依然試圖為那段距離賦予形狀。
Francis has finished singing his song. Arthur is still alive, still needs to face those questions without answers. And I, having finished this story, have not thereby obtained a definite conclusion. 弗朗西斯已經唱完了他的歌。亞瑟仍然活著，仍然需要面對那些沒有答案的問題。而我寫完這個故事，也並沒有因此獲得一個確定的結論。
The sun will keep shining. Life will keep moving forward. Those things that were never understood may not be compensated simply because they were written down. 太陽會繼續照耀。生活會繼續向前。那些未曾被理解的東西，也許不會因為被寫下來就得到補償。
This is probably the reason I wrote this story. 這大概就是我寫下這個故事的理由。
